Airway Assessment & Management

The cornerstone of resuscitation — "A" before B and C. Every critically ill patient encounter begins here.

Clinical Airway Anatomy

Upper Airway

  • • Nose & Nasopharynx — warms, humidifies, filters air. NPA placed here.
  • • Oropharynx — tongue is the #1 cause of obstruction in unconscious patients.
  • • Epiglottis — landmark for direct & video laryngoscopy. Vallecula above, arytenoids below.
  • • Hypopharynx — piriform fossae on either side. Foreign body lodgement site.

Lower Airway

  • • Larynx — vocal cords (true & false), subglottic space. Narrowest in children < 8y.
  • • Trachea — 10–12 cm long, C-shaped cartilage rings. Cricothyroid membrane at C5-6.
  • • Carina — at T4-5 (sternal angle). Right main bronchus shorter, wider, more vertical.
  • • Bronchial Tree — right mainstem intubation is the most common ETT malposition.

Difficult Airway Predictors — LEMON

  • L — Look externally: facial trauma, large tongue, short neck, obesity, beard
  • E — Evaluate 3-3-2: ≥3 fingers mouth opening, ≥3 fingers hyomental, ≥2 fingers thyro-hyoid
  • M — Mallampati: Class III/IV predict difficulty. Assess sitting, tongue out, no phonation
  • O — Obstruction: epiglottitis, peritonsillar abscess, angioedema, Ludwig's angina, tumor
  • N — Neck mobility: C-spine injury, ankylosing spondylitis, RA with C1-2 instability

⚡ Clinical Pearl

In my experience, the single most reliable predictor of a difficult airway is restricted mouth opening (< 3 cm). Mallampati alone has poor sensitivity (~50%). Always do a composite assessment. The airway that kills you is the one you didn't anticipate.

Basic Airway Maneuvers & Adjuncts

Head Tilt – Chin Lift

Gold standard for non-trauma. Extends atlanto-occipital joint, lifts tongue off posterior pharyngeal wall.

⚠ Contraindicated in suspected C-spine injury.

Jaw Thrust

Trauma-safe maneuver. Bilateral mandibular angles pushed anteriorly. Maintains neutral C-spine alignment.

✓ Use with in-line C-spine stabilization.

OPA (Guedel Airway)

Size: corner of mouth to angle of mandible. Insert inverted, rotate 180°. Only in unconscious patients — triggers gag.

✗ Never in conscious/semi-conscious → vomiting + aspiration.

NPA (Nasopharyngeal)

Size: tip of nose to tragus. Lubricate well. Insert perpendicular to face (not upward). Tolerated in semi-conscious.

✗ Relative CI: basal skull fracture (Battle's sign, raccoon eyes, CSF rhinorrhoea).

BVM (Bag-Valve-Mask)

C-E grip technique. Two-person preferred. Ensure seal before squeezing. Deliver over 1 second. Watch for chest rise.

⚡ Stomach inflation = poor seal or excessive pressure. Reposition.

Supraglottic Devices

LMA / i-gel — rescue device, intubation conduit. i-gel preferred (no cuff inflation). Size by weight.

✓ First-line rescue when intubation fails. Can ventilate while planning next step.

Preoxygenation — The Safety Net

Preoxygenation replaces nitrogen in the FRC with O₂, buying 6–8 min of apnea time in a healthy adult (as little as 90 seconds in obese/pregnant patients).

Techniques

  • 1NRB mask at 15 L/min × 3 min (8 vital-capacity breaths if emergent)
  • 2HFNC at 60–70 L/min — provides apneic oxygenation during laryngoscopy
  • 3NIV (CPAP/BiPAP) — best for obese patients, recruits atelectatic lung
  • 4BVM with PEEP valve (5–10 cmH₂O) — prevents derecruitment

⚡ From the ICU

Head-up 20–30° during preoxygenation improves FRC and safe apnea time. In obese patients, use ramped position (ear-to-sternal-notch alignment).

Apneic oxygenation: Keep nasal cannula at 15 L/min or HFNC running during intubation attempt. This alone extends safe apnea time by 2–3 minutes. I do this for every RSI.

Endotracheal Intubation

RSI, DSI, awake intubation — the definitive airway. A systematic approach saves lives.

Rapid Sequence Intubation (RSI)

CRITICAL
P1

Preparation

Equipment check: ETT (7.0–7.5♀, 8.0–8.5♂), stylet, laryngoscope (Mac 3/4 or VL), bougie, suction (Yankauer), BVM, ETCO₂, backup SGA. Two IV lines. Monitoring: SpO₂, ECG, BP (q1 min).

P2

Preoxygenation

Target EtO₂ > 85% or 3 min NRB/HFNC. Apneic oxygenation via NC at 15 L/min. Position: sniffing (ear-to-sternal notch). Head up 20-30°.

P3

Pretreatment (Selective)

Fentanyl 1–3 mcg/kg (↑ICP/reactive airway), Lidocaine 1.5 mg/kg IV (↑ICP, asthma — debatable), Atropine 0.02 mg/kg in peds < 1 year (prevent bradycardia from succinylcholine).

P4

Paralysis with Induction

Induction Agents

  • • Ketamine 1.5–2 mg/kg — hemodynamically stable, bronchodilator. My go-to in EM.
  • • Propofol 1.5–2 mg/kg — avoid in hypotension. Good for ↑ICP.
  • • Etomidate 0.3 mg/kg — hemodynamically neutral. Adrenal suppression concern (single dose likely OK).
  • • Midazolam 0.1–0.3 mg/kg — slow onset, unpredictable. Last resort.

Paralytics

  • • Succinylcholine 1.5 mg/kg — onset 45s, duration 6–10 min. Fastest. CI: hyperK, burns > 72h, crush, denervation, malignant hyperthermia hx.
  • • Rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg — onset 60s, duration 45 min. Sugammadex reversal available (16 mg/kg). Increasingly preferred.
P5

Positioning & Placement

Sniffing position. Direct laryngoscopy: Mac blade in vallecula, lift at 45° (don't lever on teeth). Video laryngoscopy: follow the curve. Cormack-Lehane Grade I/II = good view. Pass tube through cords under direct vision. ETT depth: 21 cm♀, 23 cm♂ at lip. Inflate cuff 5-10 mL.

P6

Proof of Placement

Gold standard: Waveform capnography (ETCO₂). Sustained waveform for 6 breaths confirms tracheal placement. Also: bilateral chest rise, 5-point auscultation (epigastric → L axilla → R axilla → L apex → R apex), misting of tube, SpO₂ trend. CXR to confirm depth (tip 2-4 cm above carina).

P7

Post-Intubation Management

Secure tube (tape/commercial holder). Start sedation (propofol + fentanyl or ketamine infusion). Start ventilator with lung-protective settings. Arterial line if not already placed. NGT/OGT. CXR. Post-intubation ABG at 30 min.

Video Laryngoscopy vs Direct

Video Laryngoscopy (VL)

  • ✓ Superior glottic view (especially Cormack-Lehane III/IV)
  • ✓ First-pass success rate > 95% in experienced hands
  • ✓ Better for C-spine immobilization, obesity, anterior larynx
  • ✓ Teaching tool — everyone sees the same view
  • ✗ Requires practice for tube delivery (look at screen, not mouth)
  • ✗ Blood/secretions obscure camera → always suction first

Direct Laryngoscopy (DL)

  • ✓ No technology dependency — works when VL fails/unavailable
  • ✓ Better in massive emesis/blood (direct sightline)
  • ✓ Faster in experienced hands for straightforward airways
  • ✗ Higher first-attempt failure in difficult airways
  • ✗ Requires sniffing position (may not be possible in trauma)

⚡ My practice: VL as first-line for ALL intubations. DL skill must be maintained as backup.

Mechanical Ventilation Fundamentals

Understanding the machine that breathes for your patient. Settings, physiology, and clinical reasoning.

Initial Ventilator Settings

📊 Quick IBW & Tidal Volume Calculator

IBW: 66 kg → TV: 396–528 mL

IBW (Male) = 50 + 0.91×(height cm − 152.4) | IBW (Female) = 45.5 + 0.91×(height cm − 152.4). TV = 6–8 mL/kg IBW.

Tidal Volume (Vt)

6-8 mL/kg IBW

Always use ideal body weight. Lungs don't grow with obesity. 6 mL/kg in ARDS. Never exceed 8 mL/kg.

Respiratory Rate (RR)

14-20 /min

Start 14-16. Adjust for pH/PaCO₂. In ARDS, up to 35 if needed (watch for auto-PEEP). Match minute ventilation to pre-intubation state.

PEEP

5-20 cmH₂O

Start 5 cmH₂O. ARDS: titrate by FiO₂/PEEP table or driving pressure. Higher PEEP recruits atelectatic lung but may reduce venous return.

FiO₂

21-100%

Start 100% post-intubation, then wean rapidly. Target SpO₂ 92-96% (88-92% in COPD). Prolonged FiO₂ >60% → oxygen toxicity.

I:E Ratio

1:2 to 1:4

Normal 1:2. Obstructive disease (COPD/asthma): extend to 1:3 or 1:4 for complete exhalation. Inverse ratio (2:1) rarely used in severe ARDS.

Plateau Pressure

≤ 30 cmH₂O

NEVER exceed 30. Reflects alveolar pressure. If high: reduce Vt, rule out pneumothorax, check for circuit/ETT obstruction. Driving pressure (Pplat − PEEP) < 15 is the goal.

Essential Equations

Minute Ventilation

V̇E = Vt × RR

Normal ~6 L/min. ↑ to blow off CO₂.

Static Compliance

Cst = Vt / (Pplat − PEEP)

Normal 50–80 mL/cmH₂O. Low in ARDS, fibrosis.

Driving Pressure

ΔP = Pplat − PEEP

Best predictor of ARDS mortality. Keep < 15.

P/F Ratio

PaO₂ / FiO₂

Normal > 400. Mild ARDS: 200–300. Moderate: 100–200. Severe: < 100.

Ventilator Modes

Understanding when and why to use each mode. The mode is the conversation between patient and machine.

AC

Volume Control (AC/VC)

Assist-Control / Volume-Cycled

  • Set: Vt, RR, PEEP, FiO₂, Flow rate
  • Variable: Pressure (depends on compliance & resistance)
  • Trigger: Patient or time. Every triggered breath = full Vt.
  • Use: Initial mode for most patients. Guarantees volume delivery.

⚡ Watch for: breath-stacking if RR set too high, patient-vent dyssynchrony, auto-PEEP in obstructive disease.

PC

Pressure Control (AC/PC)

Pressure-Cycled

  • Set: Inspiratory Pressure, RR, PEEP, FiO₂, I-time
  • Variable: Vt (depends on compliance & set pressure)
  • Advantage: Limits peak airway pressure. Decelerating flow → better gas distribution.
  • Use: ARDS (strict pressure control), high peak pressures on VC.

⚡ Danger: Vt NOT guaranteed. If compliance drops (mucus plug, pneumothorax), Vt drops silently. Set Vt alarms!

SIMV

SIMV (+PS)

Synchronized Intermittent Mandatory Ventilation

  • Set: Mandatory RR (guaranteed breaths) + PS level for spontaneous breaths
  • Behavior: Set number of mandatory breaths (VC or PC). Between mandatories, patient breathes spontaneously with PS support.
  • Historical use: Weaning mode — gradually reduce mandatory rate.

⚠ Largely fallen out of favor for weaning. Studies (Brochard 1994, Esteban 1995) showed PS/T-piece superior. Can cause resp muscle fatigue due to variable work of breathing between mandatory and spontaneous breaths.

PSV

Pressure Support (PSV)

Patient-Triggered, Pressure-Limited

  • Set: PS level (above PEEP), PEEP, FiO₂
  • Patient controls: RR, Vt, I-time, flow
  • Use: Weaning mode, SBT. Most comfortable for awake patients.
  • PS 5-8: Overcomes ETT resistance (used for SBT).

✓ My preferred weaning mode. PS 10-15 → wean by 2-4 q4-6h → SBT when PS 5-8 + PEEP 5 + FiO₂ ≤ 40%. Patient must have intact respiratory drive.

PRVC

PRVC

Pressure-Regulated Volume Control

  • Hybrid: Set target Vt, machine auto-adjusts pressure breath-by-breath.
  • Advantage: Guaranteed Vt with lowest possible pressure.
  • Use: Post-op, changing compliance (resolving ARDS).

⚡ Beware: if compliance improves suddenly, machine drops pressure → patient may auto-cycle. If compliance worsens, pressure rises to a set max.

APRV

APRV

Airway Pressure Release Ventilation

  • Concept: Sustained high CPAP (P-high) with brief releases (P-low) for CO₂ clearance.
  • Set: P-high (20-30), T-high (4-6s), P-low (0), T-low (0.5-0.8s)
  • T-low: Set to 75% of peak expiratory flow. This is the KEY setting.
  • Use: Severe ARDS, open-lung strategy. Patient can breathe spontaneously at P-high.

⚠ Expert mode. Requires understanding of time constants. Controversial — no mortality benefit proven over low-Vt ventilation. I use it as rescue in refractory ARDS when conventional fails.

Ventilator Adjustments Based on ABG

↑ PaCO₂ (Respiratory Acidosis)

Patient not ventilating enough

Action: ↑ Minute ventilation

• ↑ RR (first choice, up to 35)

• ↑ Vt (if Pplat allows, stay ≤ 30)

⚡ In ARDS: accept permissive hypercapnia (pH > 7.20) rather than injure lungs with high Vt.

↓ PaCO₂ (Respiratory Alkalosis)

Over-ventilation

Action: ↓ Minute ventilation

• ↓ RR first

• ↓ Vt if appropriate

⚡ Exception: therapeutic hyperventilation for ↑ICP (target PaCO₂ 30-35, short-term bridge only).

↓ PaO₂ (Hypoxemia)

Oxygenation failure

Action: Improve oxygenation

• ↑ FiO₂ (rapid fix)

• ↑ PEEP (recruits alveoli)

• ↑ I-time / mean airway pressure

⚡ Remember: CO₂ = ventilation problem (RR/Vt). O₂ = oxygenation problem (FiO₂/PEEP). Different knobs!

Ventilator Emergencies

When the alarms scream — systematic approach to the crashing ventilated patient.

DOPES Mnemonic — Acute Desaturation

MEMORIZE THIS

Step 1: Disconnect from ventilator. Bag with 100% O₂. If patient improves → machine problem. If not → patient problem.

D

Displacement

ETT dislodged (extubation), right mainstem, or esophageal. Check ETCO₂ waveform, tube depth at lip, bilateral breath sounds. If in doubt → direct laryngoscopy to confirm tube in trachea.

O

Obstruction

Mucus plug, blood clot, patient biting tube. Pass suction catheter. If catheter won't pass → kinked tube or impacted plug. Consider bronchoscopy or reintubation. Use bite block.

P

Pneumothorax

Tension pneumothorax — the killer. Absent breath sounds unilaterally, tracheal deviation, hypotension, distended neck veins, ↑ peak pressures. Don't wait for CXR. Needle decompression 2nd ICS MCL → then chest tube. Clinical diagnosis, clinical treatment.

E

Equipment

Circuit disconnect, O₂ supply failure, ventilator malfunction, cuff leak. Check all connections. Listen for air leak. Check cuff pressure (20-30 cmH₂O). If ventilator suspect → switch to backup or manual bag.

S

Stacking (Breath) / Special

Auto-PEEP / breath stacking — especially asthma/COPD. Disconnect, allow full exhalation (6-10 seconds). Also consider: bronchospasm (albuterol), PE (heparin), MI (ECG/troponin), anaphylaxis, abdominal compartment syndrome.

Common Alarm Troubleshooting

High Peak Pressure

  • → If Pplat also high: ↓ compliance (pneumothorax, atelectasis, pulmonary edema, ARDS, abdominal distension)
  • → If Pplat normal but Peak high: ↑ resistance (bronchospasm, mucus plug, kinked tube, biting, small ETT)
  • → Check Peak-Pplat gradient. > 5-10 = resistance problem.

Low Minute Ventilation

  • → Circuit leak (check connections, cuff pressure)
  • → ↓ patient effort (over-sedation, fatigue, neurologic event)
  • → Chest tube with large air leak (bronchopleural fistula)

Liberation & Weaning

Getting the patient off the ventilator — the goal from day one. "The best ventilator setting is OFF."

Readiness Criteria for SBT

✓ Must Meet ALL

  • • Cause of respiratory failure improving/resolved
  • • FiO₂ ≤ 40%, PEEP ≤ 5-8 cmH₂O
  • • P/F ratio > 150-200
  • • Hemodynamically stable (no/low vasopressors)
  • • Adequate mental status (follows commands or baseline)
  • • Cough reflex present
  • • Manageable secretions
  • • No planned surgery/procedures

📊 Weaning Predictors

  • RSBI (f/Vt): < 105 predicts success. RR/Vt in liters during 1-min spontaneous breathing. Best single predictor.
  • NIF (MIP): ≤ −20 to −25 cmH₂O (adequate diaphragm strength)
  • Vt: > 5 mL/kg spontaneous
  • Vital Capacity: > 10 mL/kg
  • Minute Ventilation: < 10 L/min

Spontaneous Breathing Trial (SBT)

Methods

  • PS 5-8 + PEEP 5 — most common. PS overcomes ETT resistance. My standard approach.
  • T-piece — complete disconnect. No support. More demanding. If passes → very likely to succeed extubation.
  • CPAP 5 — minimal support. Similar to T-piece but with alarms.

Duration: 30 minutes is sufficient (ATS/ACCP 2017). 120 min adds no predictive value.

SBT Failure Criteria — Stop if:

  • ✗ RR > 35 for > 5 minutes
  • ✗ SpO₂ < 90%
  • ✗ HR > 140 or Δ > 20% from baseline
  • ✗ SBP > 180 or < 90 mmHg
  • ✗ Diaphoresis, agitation, anxiety
  • ✗ Accessory muscle use, paradoxical breathing
  • ✗ Arrhythmia

⚡ Failed SBT? Rest 24h on comfortable settings, investigate cause (fluid overload, delirium, weakness, unresolved pathology), then retry.

Extubation & Post-Extubation Care

Pre-Extubation Checklist

  • ☐ Cuff leak test (if concern for laryngeal edema: female, prolonged intubation > 6 days, traumatic intubation, repeat intubation)
  • ☐ Head up 30-45°
  • ☐ Suction oral and subglottic secretions
  • ☐ Equipment at bedside (reintubation kit, NIPPV, HFNC)
  • ☐ IV dexamethasone 8 mg if failed cuff leak (give 4-12h before extubation)
  • ☐ Patient NPO or minimal PO

Post-Extubation Support

  • • Low risk: Standard O₂ (NC, Venturi)
  • • High risk of failure: Prophylactic NIV (COPD, CHF, obesity, age > 65) — reduces reintubation by 50%
  • • HFNC: 40-60 L/min. Non-inferior to NIV for preventing reintubation. Better tolerated.
  • • Monitor for 24-48h. Reintubation rate ~15%. Most failures in first 48h.

✓ My practice: HFNC post-extubation for all ICU patients. Prophylactic NIV for COPD/CHF. Daily SAT + SBT protocol (Wake Up & Breathe).
