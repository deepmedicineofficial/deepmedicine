Sepsis-3 Definitions (2016)

SEPSIS

Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection.

Clinical Criteria Suspected infection + SOFA score ≥ 2 points (or acute change of ≥ 2)

SEPTIC SHOCK

Subset of sepsis with profound circulatory, cellular, and metabolic abnormalities.

Clinical Criteria Vasopressors required for MAP ≥ 65 mmHg
AND Lactate > 2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation
Mortality: 40–60%

Pathophysiology of Sepsis

The Sepsis Cascade

1

Infection & PAMP/DAMP Recognition

Pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) — e.g., LPS, lipoteichoic acid, peptidoglycan — bind to Toll-like receptors (TLR-2, TLR-4) and NOD-like receptors on innate immune cells. Damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) — HMGB1, histones, mitochondrial DNA — amplify the response from tissue injury.

2

Cytokine Storm & NF-κB Activation

NF-κB pathway activation triggers massive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines: TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, IFN-γ. Simultaneously, anti-inflammatory mediators (IL-10, TGF-β) cause immunoparalysis. This imbalance creates a "cytokine storm" with uncontrolled systemic inflammation.

3

Endothelial Dysfunction & Coagulopathy

Endothelial glycocalyx degradation → increased vascular permeability → third-spacing → tissue edema. Tissue factor exposure activates extrinsic coagulation cascade. Protein C/S and antithrombin III depletion → microvascular thrombosis (DIC). Loss of autoregulation in multiple vascular beds.

4

Mitochondrial Dysfunction & Cellular Injury

Reactive oxygen/nitrogen species (ROS/RNS) damage electron transport chain → cytopathic hypoxia. Cells cannot utilize oxygen despite adequate delivery ("oxygen extraction deficit"). Mitochondrial membrane potential collapse → caspase activation → apoptosis of immune cells, endothelial cells, and parenchymal cells.

5

Multi-Organ Dysfunction Syndrome (MODS)

Distributive shock (vasodilation + capillary leak) → hypotension. Myocardial depression (septic cardiomyopathy) → reduced EF. ARDS from alveolar-capillary membrane damage. AKI from renal hypoperfusion + direct tubular injury. Hepatic dysfunction, encephalopathy, ileus, and adrenal insufficiency.

Key Inflammatory Mediators

Pro-Inflammatory

TNF-α: First-wave cytokine; causes fever, vasodilation, endothelial activation, apoptosis
IL-1β: Synergistic with TNF-α; activates inflammasome, pyrogen
IL-6: Acute phase response; CRP induction; B-cell differentiation
IL-8 (CXCL8): Neutrophil chemotaxis and activation
HMGB1: Late mediator; perpetuates inflammation; therapeutic target
Complement (C3a, C5a): Anaphylatoxins, neutrophil activation, mast cell degranulation

Anti-Inflammatory / Immunosuppressive

IL-10: Potent immunosuppressant; inhibits macrophage function
TGF-β: Promotes fibrosis; suppresses T-cell responses
IL-4 / IL-13: Th2 shift; reduced pathogen clearance
PD-1 / PD-L1: T-cell exhaustion markers; checkpoint pathway
Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells: Expand in sepsis; suppress adaptive immunity
Lymphocyte Apoptosis: Massive depletion → immunoparalysis phase

Diagnostic Approach

STAT / Immediate

  • • CBC with differential (left shift, bandemia)
  • • CMP: BUN, Creatinine, Electrolytes, LFTs
  • • Lactate (arterial or venous) — KEY prognostic marker
  • • ABG with co-oximetry
  • • Coagulation: PT/INR, aPTT, Fibrinogen, D-dimer
  • • Blood cultures × 2 sets (before antibiotics!)
  • • Urinalysis + Urine culture
  • • Blood glucose

Biomarkers

  • • Procalcitonin (PCT): >0.5 ng/mL suggests bacterial; guides de-escalation
  • • CRP: Non-specific; trend useful for monitoring
  • • Presepsin (sCD14-ST): Rising biomarker for early sepsis
  • • IL-6: Rapid rise, correlates with severity
  • • sTREM-1: Elevated in bacterial infections
  • • ProADM: Predicts organ failure progression

Source-Directed

  • • Sputum Gram stain + culture, respiratory viral panel
  • • CSF analysis if meningitis suspected (cell count, protein, glucose, Gram stain, culture, latex agglutination)
  • • Wound/abscess cultures, joint fluid analysis
  • • Stool studies (C. difficile toxin) if diarrhea present
  • • β-D-glucan, Galactomannan if invasive fungal infection suspected

Chest X-ray / CT Chest

Pneumonia, pleural effusion, empyema, ARDS pattern. CT for cavitation, abscess, mediastinitis.

CT Abdomen/Pelvis

Intra-abdominal abscess, perforated viscus, biliary obstruction, appendicitis, diverticular abscess.

Point-of-Care Ultrasound

IVC collapsibility (volume status), cardiac function (EF, RV), lung B-lines, free fluid (FAST), abscess localization.

Echocardiography

Septic cardiomyopathy (global hypokinesis), vegetations (endocarditis), fluid responsiveness assessment.

Management Protocol

HOUR-1 BUNDLE (SSC 2021)

Initiate ALL elements IMMEDIATELY — do not wait for ICU transfer

1

Measure Lactate

If lactate ≥ 2 mmol/L, remeasure within 2–4 hours. Target: lactate normalization. Serial trending guides response to therapy.

2

Obtain Blood Cultures BEFORE Antibiotics

At least 2 sets (aerobic + anaerobic) from 2 different sites. Do NOT delay antibiotics > 45 minutes for cultures.

3

Administer Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics

Within 1 hour of recognition. Each hour delay increases mortality ~7.6%. Cover all likely pathogens. IV route. Loading doses independent of renal function.

4

Rapid IV Fluid Resuscitation

30 mL/kg crystalloid (Ringer's Lactate or NS) for hypotension or lactate ≥ 4. Reassess volume status after each bolus using dynamic parameters (pulse pressure variation, passive leg raise, IVC assessment).

5

Vasopressors for Refractory Hypotension

If MAP < 65 mmHg despite fluids → Norepinephrine (first-line, 0.1–2 µg/kg/min). Add Vasopressin 0.03 U/min as second agent. Epinephrine if cardiac dysfunction suspected.

Hemodynamic Targets & Monitoring

Targets

  • • MAP ≥ 65 mmHg
  • • Urine output ≥ 0.5 mL/kg/hr
  • • Lactate clearance ≥ 20% in 2 hrs
  • • ScvO₂ ≥ 70% (if available)
  • • Capillary refill < 3 seconds

Fluid Responsiveness Assessment

  • • Passive Leg Raise (+10% CO = responsive)
  • • Pulse Pressure Variation > 13%
  • • IVC collapsibility > 40% (spontaneous)
  • • Mini fluid challenge (100 mL over 1 min)
  • • Avoid fluid overload → ↑ mortality

Vasopressor Ladder

  • 1st: Norepinephrine (NE) — α1 > β1
  • 2nd: Vasopressin 0.03 U/min (NE-sparing)
  • 3rd: Epinephrine (cardiac dysfunction)
  • Rescue: Angiotensin II (ATHOS-3 trial)
  • Adjunct: Hydrocortisone 200 mg/day if refractory

Adjunctive & Supportive Therapies

Corticosteroids

Hydrocortisone 50 mg IV q6h (or 200 mg continuous infusion) if vasopressor-refractory shock (NE ≥ 0.25 µg/kg/min for ≥ 4 hours). ADRENAL & APROCCHSS trials support use. Taper when vasopressors weaned.

Glycemic Control

Target glucose 144–180 mg/dL (insulin infusion protocol). Avoid hypoglycemia (< 70 mg/dL) — associated with increased mortality. Check glucose q1–2h during insulin infusion.

Lung-Protective Ventilation

If ARDS: Tidal volume 6 mL/kg IBW, Plateau pressure < 30 cmH₂O, PEEP per FiO₂ table, prone positioning if PaO₂/FiO₂ < 150 for ≥ 12 hours. Conservative fluid strategy once stabilized.

Source Control

Identify and intervene within 6–12 hours: abscess drainage, infected device removal, debridement of necrotic tissue, biliary decompression (ERCP/PTC), fasciotomy for necrotizing fasciitis. Choose least invasive effective approach.

VTE Prophylaxis

LMWH preferred over UFH (unless AKI/high bleeding risk). Enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily or Heparin 5000 U SC q8-12h. Mechanical prophylaxis if pharmacological contraindicated.

Renal Replacement Therapy

Indications: refractory hyperkalemia, severe metabolic acidosis, fluid overload unresponsive to diuretics, uremic complications. CRRT preferred in hemodynamically unstable patients. STARRT-ART trial: no benefit of early initiation.

Empiric Antibiotic Selection

Principles: Administer within 1 hour. Use IV route. Loading doses are NOT adjusted for renal function. Broaden if MRSA/MDR risk factors present. De-escalate based on culture results and procalcitonin.

Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)

Non-severe (ward): β-Lactam (Ampicillin/Sulbactam or Ceftriaxone) + Macrolide (Azithromycin) OR Respiratory FQ (Levofloxacin/Moxifloxacin)
Severe/ICU: Ceftriaxone 2g IV + Azithromycin 500mg IV. Add Vancomycin + Piperacillin-Tazobactam if MRSA/Pseudomonas risk. Consider Oseltamivir if influenza season.

Hospital-Acquired / Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia

Standard: Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5g IV q6h OR Cefepime 2g IV q8h OR Meropenem 1g IV q8h
High MDR risk: Two anti-pseudomonal agents + MRSA coverage (Vancomycin 25–30 mg/kg load or Linezolid 600mg IV q12h)

Urosepsis / Complicated UTI

Community-onset: Ceftriaxone 2g IV daily OR Piperacillin-Tazobactam. Add Aminoglycoside (Gentamicin) if critically ill.
ESBL/MDR suspected: Meropenem 1g IV q8h or Ertapenem 1g IV daily. Consider source control (ureteral stent, nephrostomy) if obstructed.

Intra-Abdominal Sepsis

Regimens: Piperacillin-Tazobactam 4.5g IV q6h OR Meropenem 1g IV q8h OR Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole. Add Fluconazole/Caspofungin if yeast suspected (tertiary peritonitis). Source control is paramount — surgery/IR drainage within 6–12 hours.

Bacterial Meningitis

Adult empiric: Ceftriaxone 2g IV q12h + Vancomycin 15–20 mg/kg IV q8–12h + Ampicillin 2g IV q4h (if age >50 or immunocompromised — covers Listeria). Dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg IV q6h × 4 days — give BEFORE or WITH first antibiotic dose (reduces mortality in pneumococcal meningitis).

Necrotizing Soft Tissue Infection

Triple therapy: Vancomycin (MRSA) + Piperacillin-Tazobactam or Meropenem (gram-negatives/anaerobes) + Clindamycin 900mg IV q8h (toxin suppression — inhibits protein synthesis, blocks superantigen production). EMERGENT SURGICAL DEBRIDEMENT IS LIFE-SAVING. Re-explore in 24–48h. LRINEC score ≥ 6 raises suspicion.

Special Topics

Special Populations

Immunocompromised Patients

  • • Broader empiric coverage — add antifungals early
  • • Consider atypical pathogens: PJP, CMV, Aspergillus
  • • Neutropenic sepsis: anti-pseudomonal β-lactam ± Vancomycin
  • • G-CSF if ANC < 500 with expected prolonged neutropenia
  • • Lower threshold for CT imaging (may lack inflammatory signs)

Elderly Patients (>65 years)

  • • Atypical presentation: confusion may be ONLY sign
  • • Blunted febrile response (hypothermia common)
  • • Lower physiologic reserve → rapid decompensation
  • • Polypharmacy interactions (anticoagulants, antihypertensives)
  • • Discuss goals of care and advance directives early

Obstetric Sepsis

  • • Leading cause of maternal mortality worldwide
  • • Sources: chorioamnionitis, endometritis, pyelonephritis
  • • Normal pregnancy hemodynamics mask early shock
  • • Modified SOFA for pregnancy; lower BP thresholds
  • • Fetal monitoring alongside maternal resuscitation

Burns & Trauma Sepsis

  • • Baseline SIRS from tissue injury confounds diagnosis
  • • Burn wound sepsis: quantitative culture >10⁵ organisms/g
  • • Increased drug clearance → higher antibiotic doses needed
  • • Aggressive wound care + early excision/grafting
  • • Higher metabolic demands → enhanced nutritional support

Quality Metrics & Prognosis

Bundle Compliance Impact

  • • 3-hour bundle compliance: ↓ mortality by 15–20%
  • • Each hour delay in antibiotics: ↑ mortality ~7.6%
  • • Complete Hour-1 bundle: NNT = 6 to save one life
  • • Lactate-guided resuscitation: ↓ mortality by 11%
  • • Procalcitonin-guided de-escalation: ↓ antibiotic days by 2–3

Prognostic Factors (Poor Outcome)

  • • Lactate > 4 mmol/L that fails to clear
  • • Need for ≥ 2 vasopressors
  • • SOFA score > 11
  • • Thrombocytopenia (DIC)
  • • Immunosuppression / Malignancy
  • • Delay in source control > 12 hours
  • • MDR pathogen isolation

Post-Sepsis Syndrome & Long-Term Outcomes

Physical

  • • ICU-acquired weakness (CIM/CIP)
  • • Chronic pain and fatigue
  • • Recurrent infections
  • • Organ dysfunction persistence

Cognitive & Psychological

  • • Cognitive impairment (30–50%)
  • • PTSD (up to 44%)
  • • Depression & anxiety
  • • Sleep disturbances

Healthcare Utilization

  • • 40% rehospitalized within 90 days
  • • 60% mortality within 2 years (elderly)
  • • Higher cardiovascular event risk
  • • Need for rehabilitation services
