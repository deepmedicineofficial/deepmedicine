Advanced Cardiovascular Life Support — AHA 2020/2025 Guidelines

CRITICAL Adult Cardiac Arrest Algorithm

AHA 2020 Guidelines — Assess rhythm every 2 minutes. Minimize interruptions in chest compressions.

1. Confirm Cardiac Arrest

  • Unresponsive, no breathing or only gasping
  • No definite pulse within 10 seconds
  • Activate Emergency Response / Call for defibrillator

2. Start High-Quality CPR

  • Rate: 100–120 compressions/min
  • Depth: At least 2 inches (5 cm), not exceeding 2.4 inches (6 cm)
  • Recoil: Allow complete chest recoil between compressions
  • Ratio: 30:2 (no advanced airway) or continuous (with advanced airway, 1 breath every 6 sec)
  • Minimize interruptions: <10 sec for rhythm checks
  • Rotate compressors every 2 minutes

3. Rhythm Analysis — Shockable?

VF / Pulseless VT (Shockable)

  • Defibrillate: Biphasic 120–200 J (or manufacturer dose); Monophasic 360 J
  • Resume CPR immediately × 2 min
  • If refractory after 2nd shock → Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO q3–5 min
  • After 3rd shock → Amiodarone 300 mg IV/IO bolus (2nd dose: 150 mg) OR Lidocaine 1–1.5 mg/kg (then 0.5–0.75 mg/kg)
  • Consider: Double sequential defibrillation for refractory VF

PEA / Asystole (Non-Shockable)

  • Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO ASAP then q3–5 min
  • Resume CPR × 2 min
  • Recheck rhythm every 2 min
  • If organized rhythm → check pulse
  • Aggressively search for reversible causes (H's & T's)
  • Consider: Advanced airway, waveform capnography (ETCO₂ >10 mmHg target)

4. During CPR — Continuous Reassessment

  • Waveform capnography: ETCO₂ <10 mmHg = improve CPR quality
  • IV/IO access
  • Advanced airway (ETT or supraglottic) — do not delay compressions
  • Treat reversible causes (H's & T's)
  • If ROSC → go to Post-Cardiac Arrest Care

UNSTABLE Adult Bradycardia Algorithm

HR <60 bpm with inadequate perfusion. Identify and treat underlying cause.

1. Assess Adequacy of Perfusion

  • Signs/symptoms: Hypotension, altered mental status, chest pain, acute heart failure, shock
  • If adequate perfusion → observe and monitor
  • If signs of poor perfusion → treat immediately

2. First-Line: Atropine

  • Atropine 1 mg IV every 3–5 min (max 3 mg total)
  • May be ineffective in: Mobitz Type II, 3rd degree AV block, infra-nodal block
  • Do NOT rely on atropine for transplanted hearts (denervated)

3. If Atropine Ineffective

Transcutaneous Pacing

  • Start at 60–80 mA, increase until capture
  • Rate 60–80 bpm
  • Verify mechanical capture (pulse)
  • Consider sedation/analgesia

Chronotropic Infusions

  • Dopamine: 5–20 mcg/kg/min IV
  • Epinephrine: 2–10 mcg/min IV
  • Isoproterenol: 2–10 mcg/min (if available)

4. Definitive: Transvenous Pacing

  • Consult cardiology for transvenous pacemaker
  • Consider permanent pacemaker if cause not reversible
Key Bradycardia Differentials

Sinus Bradycardia: Normal P waves, regular, responds to atropine

1° AV Block: PR >200 ms, usually benign, observe

Mobitz Type I (Wenckebach): Progressive PR prolongation → dropped beat. Usually benign.

Mobitz Type II: Fixed PR then sudden drop. HIGH risk of progression → pace early.

3° (Complete) AV Block: AV dissociation. Wide escape = unstable. Pace immediately.

Drug-induced: Beta-blockers, CCBs, digoxin, amiodarone. Treat cause + temporize.

CRITICAL Adult Tachycardia with Pulse Algorithm

HR >150 bpm usually symptomatic. Assess stability first.

1. Assess Hemodynamic Stability

UNSTABLE (any of the following):

Hypotension, acute AMS, chest pain, acute HF → Immediate synchronized cardioversion

  • Narrow regular: 50–100 J
  • Narrow irregular (AFib): 120–200 J biphasic
  • Wide regular: 100 J
  • Wide irregular: Defibrillation (treat as VF) — DO NOT synchronize

2. Stable — Determine QRS Width

Narrow QRS (≤0.12 sec)

Regular:

  • Vagal maneuvers (modified Valsalva, carotid sinus massage)
  • Adenosine 6 mg rapid IV push → if no effect → 12 mg → may repeat 12 mg once
  • If SVT: Adenosine is diagnostic AND therapeutic
  • If adenosine fails: CCB (diltiazem 15–20 mg IV) or BB (metoprolol 5 mg IV)

Irregular (likely AFib/AFlutter):

  • Rate control: Diltiazem, BB, or digoxin
  • Consider anticoagulation (CHA₂DS₂-VASc)
  • If <48 hrs: may cardiovert with anticoagulation

Wide QRS (>0.12 sec)

Regular (VT or uncertain):

  • Amiodarone 150 mg IV over 10 min, may repeat; then 1 mg/min × 6 hrs
  • OR Procainamide 20–50 mg/min (max 17 mg/kg); stop if QRS widens >50% or hypotension
  • OR Sotalol 100 mg (1.5 mg/kg) IV over 5 min
  • If definitely SVT with aberrancy → treat as narrow complex
  • If uncertain: treat as VT

Irregular (AFib with WPW or polymorphic VT):

  • AFib + WPW: AVOID AV nodal blockers (adenosine, CCB, digoxin, BB) — use procainamide or cardiovert
  • Polymorphic VT (Torsades): Magnesium 1–2 g IV, defibrillate if unstable, overdrive pacing

ROSC Post-Cardiac Arrest Care

Systematic approach after return of spontaneous circulation. Focus on organ preservation.

1. Airway & Ventilation

  • Secure advanced airway (ETT preferred); confirm placement with waveform capnography
  • Target SpO₂ 92–98% — avoid hyperoxia (titrate FiO₂ down)
  • Target PaCO₂ 35–45 mmHg — avoid hyperventilation
  • Ventilator: RR 10–12, TV 6–8 mL/kg IBW

2. Hemodynamic Optimization

  • Target SBP ≥90 mmHg / MAP ≥65 mmHg
  • IV fluid bolus if hypovolemic
  • Vasopressors: Norepinephrine (first-line) or epinephrine infusion
  • 12-lead ECG ASAP — evaluate for STEMI
  • STEMI or high suspicion of coronary occlusion → emergent PCI (regardless of coma status)
  • Echocardiography to assess LV function, RV strain, tamponade

3. Targeted Temperature Management (TTM)

  • Target: 32–36°C for ≥24 hours (select constant temperature)
  • Indication: Comatose patients (GCS <8) after ROSC from any initial rhythm
  • Methods: Surface cooling, intravascular cooling devices, cold IV fluids (adjunct only)
  • Actively prevent fever (>37.7°C) for at least 72 hours
  • Rewarm slowly: 0.25–0.5°C per hour
  • Monitor: Core temp, shivering (manage with sedation/paralysis), electrolytes (K⁺, Mg²⁺)

4. Neuroprognostication

  • Wait ≥72 hours after ROSC (or rewarming) before prognostication
  • Multimodal approach: Clinical exam, EEG, SSEP, NSE, MRI brain
  • Absent pupillary/corneal reflexes at ≥72 hrs = poor prognosis indicator
  • No single test should be used in isolation

5. Seizure Management & General ICU Care

  • Continuous EEG monitoring recommended
  • Treat clinical seizures: Levetiracetam, valproate, benzodiazepines
  • Glucose: Target 140–180 mg/dL (avoid hypoglycemia)
  • Prevent and treat: Myoclonus, infections, DVT prophylaxis
ACLS Pharmacology Quick Reference

Epinephrine (Adrenaline)

Cardiac Arrest: 1 mg IV/IO every 3–5 min

Bradycardia/Post-ROSC infusion: 2–10 mcg/min IV

Anaphylaxis: 0.3–0.5 mg IM (1:1000)

MOA: α₁ (vasoconstriction → ↑CPP) + β₁ (↑HR, contractility)

Amiodarone

VF/pVT Arrest: 300 mg IV bolus → 150 mg (2nd dose)

Stable VT: 150 mg IV over 10 min → 1 mg/min × 6 hrs → 0.5 mg/min × 18 hrs

MOA: Class III (K⁺ channel block) + Class I, II, IV properties. Prolongs AP & refractoriness.

Caution: Hypotension, bradycardia, QT prolongation. Contraindicated with other QT-prolonging agents.

Lidocaine

VF/pVT Arrest: 1–1.5 mg/kg IV → 0.5–0.75 mg/kg q5–10 min (max 3 mg/kg)

Maintenance: 1–4 mg/min infusion

MOA: Class IB Na⁺ channel blocker. Alternative to amiodarone.

Adenosine

SVT: 6 mg rapid IV push → 12 mg → 12 mg (each followed by 20 mL NS flush + arm elevation)

MOA: A₁ receptor → ↓AV node conduction. Half-life <10 sec. Administer via proximal/central line preferred.

Caution: Bronchospasm (relative CI in asthma). Reduce dose if on carbamazepine or transplant (start 3 mg). Increase dose if on caffeine/theophylline.

Atropine

Bradycardia: 1 mg IV every 3–5 min (max 3 mg)

MOA: Muscarinic (M₂) antagonist → ↑SA node firing, ↑AV conduction. Ineffective in infranodal block.

Magnesium Sulfate

Torsades de Pointes: 1–2 g IV over 5–20 min (dilute in 10 mL D5W)

Cardiac Arrest (Torsades): 1–2 g IV push

MOA: Membrane stabilizer, suppresses EADs, corrects hypomagnesemia.

Procainamide

Stable Wide-Complex Tachycardia: 20–50 mg/min IV until: arrhythmia suppressed, hypotension, QRS widens >50%, or max dose 17 mg/kg

Maintenance: 1–4 mg/min

MOA: Class IA Na⁺ channel blocker. Preferred in WPW + AFib. Avoid with QT prolongation or CHF.

Dopamine

Bradycardia: 5–20 mcg/kg/min IV infusion (chronotropic)

Dose-dependent: 1–5 (renal), 5–10 (β₁ cardiac), 10–20 (α₁ vasopressor). Alternative to pacing bridge.

Calcium Chloride / Gluconate

Hyperkalemia, Ca²⁺ channel blocker OD, Hypocalcemia:

CaCl₂ 10%: 500–1000 mg (5–10 mL) IV over 2–5 min (central line preferred)

Ca Gluconate: 1–3 g IV (peripheral safe)

MOA: Membrane stabilizer. Does NOT lower K⁺ — only antagonizes cardiac effects.

Sodium Bicarbonate

Hyperkalemia, TCA OD, severe metabolic acidosis: 1 mEq/kg IV

NOT recommended as routine in cardiac arrest. Consider if pre-existing metabolic acidosis, hyperK⁺, or TCA poisoning.

Reversible Causes — The H's & T's

Search aggressively during CPR. Treating the underlying cause is essential for ROSC, especially in PEA/Asystole.

The H's

Hypovolemia

Signs: Narrow-complex PEA, flat neck veins, hx trauma/bleeding

Rx: Volume resuscitation (crystalloid, blood products), source control

Hypoxia

Signs: Cyanosis, prior respiratory distress, low SpO₂

Rx: Advanced airway, confirm placement, 100% O₂, bilateral chest rise

Hydrogen Ion (Acidosis)

Signs: Prolonged arrest, DKA, renal failure, sepsis, drug OD

Rx: NaHCO₃ 1 mEq/kg IV, hyperventilation, treat underlying cause

Hyperkalemia / Hypokalemia

Signs: Peaked T waves, wide QRS, sine wave (hyperK); flat T, U waves (hypoK)

Rx HyperK: CaCl₂, NaHCO₃, insulin+glucose, albuterol, kayexalate, dialysis

Rx HypoK: IV KCl replacement (10–20 mEq/hr, max 40 mEq/hr with monitoring)

Hypothermia

Signs: Environmental exposure, core temp <30°C, Osborn (J) waves

Rx: Active rewarming (warm fluids, blankets, ECMO if severe). May need prolonged resuscitation. Defibrillation may fail below 30°C.

The T's

Tension Pneumothorax

Signs: Absent breath sounds unilateral, tracheal deviation, JVD, hypotension

Rx: Needle decompression (2nd ICS MCL or 4th/5th ICS AAL) → chest tube

Tamponade (Cardiac)

Signs: Beck's triad (JVD, hypotension, muffled heart sounds), PEA, narrow pulse pressure

Rx: Pericardiocentesis (subxiphoid approach, echo-guided), emergent thoracotomy

Toxins / Drug Overdose

Signs: Toxidromes, medication bottles, track marks, wide QRS (TCA), opioid pupils

Rx: Specific antidotes — Naloxone (opioid), NaHCO₃ (TCA), Flumazenil (benzo with caution), Digibind (digoxin), Lipid emulsion (local anesthetic toxicity)

Thrombosis — Coronary (MI)

Signs: Prior CP, ST changes, risk factors, known CAD

Rx: Emergent PCI (if available within protocol), fibrinolytics (if PCI unavailable in arrest → consider empiric tenecteplase)

Thrombosis — Pulmonary (PE)

Signs: Sudden PEA, prior DVT/immobility, RV dilation on echo, known hypercoagulable state

Rx: Systemic thrombolytics (alteplase 50 mg IV bolus in arrest), surgical embolectomy, ECMO. Continue CPR 60–90 min post-lytic.
