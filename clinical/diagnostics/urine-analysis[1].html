Urine Analysis

Complete Clinical Pathology Reference

Volume

Normal (Adult) 600–2000 mL/24h
Average 1200–1500 mL/24h
Oliguria < 400 mL/24h
Anuria < 100 mL/24h
Polyuria > 3000 mL/24h

Color

Normal Pale yellow to amber (Urochrome)
Red/Pink Hematuria, hemoglobinuria, myoglobinuria, beets, rifampicin
Dark brown Bilirubin (hepatitis), myoglobin, melanin
Orange Bilirubin, pyridium, dehydration
Green/Blue Pseudomonas UTI, methylene blue, amitriptyline
Black Alkaptonuria (homogentisic acid), melanoma

Appearance / Turbidity

Normal Clear to slightly hazy
Turbid causes WBCs, RBCs, bacteria, crystals, epithelial cells, lipids, mucus
Milky Chyluria (filariasis), lipiduria (nephrotic syndrome)

Odor & Specific Gravity

Normal odor Faintly aromatic
Ammonia Old standing specimen, UTI (urea-splitting organisms)
Fruity/Sweet Diabetic ketoacidosis (acetone)
Mousy/Musty Phenylketonuria
Maple syrup Maple syrup urine disease (branched-chain aminoaciduria)
Sp. Gravity (normal) 1.003–1.030 (random); 1.015–1.025 (24h)
Isosthenuria Fixed at 1.010 (renal failure, loss of concentrating ability)
Hyposthenuria < 1.007 (Diabetes insipidus, overhydration)

pH

Normal range: 4.5–8.0 (average 6.0, slightly acidic)
Acidic urine: High protein diet, DKA, starvation, diarrhea, cranberries, ammonium chloride
Alkaline urine: Vegetarian diet, UTI (Proteus), vomiting, RTA, old specimens, bicarbonate therapy

Protein

Normal < 150 mg/24h (albumin < 30 mg/24h)
Dipstick detects Primarily albumin (≥30 mg/dL)
Microalbuminuria 30–300 mg/24h (early diabetic nephropathy)
Nephrotic range > 3.5 g/24h
Bence Jones protein Light chains (multiple myeloma) — NOT detected by dipstick; use SSA/heat test
Confirmatory tests Sulfosalicylic acid (SSA), heat & acetic acid test, 24h urine protein
False positive Highly alkaline urine (pH > 8), concentrated specimen, quaternary ammonium compounds

Glucose

Normal Negative (renal threshold ~180 mg/dL blood glucose)
Dipstick method Glucose oxidase (specific for glucose)
Glycosuria causes Diabetes mellitus, pregnancy, Fanconi syndrome, renal glycosuria
False negative Ascorbic acid (vitamin C), high specific gravity
Benedict's test Non-specific reducing substance test (detects galactose, fructose, lactose also)

Ketones

Normal Negative
Types Acetoacetate (detected by dipstick), acetone, β-hydroxybutyrate (most abundant but NOT detected by dipstick)
Dipstick reaction Sodium nitroprusside (Legal's test principle)
Causes DKA, starvation, high-fat diet, pregnancy (hyperemesis), alcoholic ketoacidosis, glycogen storage diseases

Blood (Hemoglobin)

Normal Negative
Dipstick detects Free hemoglobin, intact RBCs, myoglobin (peroxidase activity)
Pattern Uniform color = free Hb/myoglobin; speckled = intact RBCs
False positive Menstrual contamination, oxidizing agents, bacterial peroxidases
False negative Ascorbic acid, formalin, high specific gravity, captopril

Bilirubin & Urobilinogen

Bilirubin normal Negative (only conjugated/direct bilirubin appears in urine)
↑ Bilirubin Obstructive jaundice, hepatocellular disease
Urobilinogen normal 0.2–1.0 mg/dL (Ehrlich units)
↑ Urobilinogen Hemolytic anemia, hepatitis
↓ / Absent urobilinogen Complete biliary obstruction, broad-spectrum antibiotics
Ictotest Confirmatory for bilirubin (diazo tablet test)

Nitrite & Leukocyte Esterase

Nitrite normal Negative
Nitrite positive Gram-negative bacteria (E. coli, Klebsiella, Proteus) reduce nitrate → nitrite
False negative nitrite Gram-positive organisms, insufficient bladder incubation (< 4h), ascorbic acid, low dietary nitrate
LE normal Negative
LE positive Pyuria / WBCs (UTI, interstitial nephritis)
Both positive Strong indicator of bacterial UTI

Cells

RBCs normal 0–2 /HPF
Dysmorphic RBCs Glomerular origin (acanthocytes, GBM damage)
Isomorphic RBCs Non-glomerular (stones, trauma, tumor)
WBCs normal 0–5 /HPF
Pyuria > 5 WBC/HPF (UTI, interstitial nephritis, TB)
Eosinophiluria Allergic interstitial nephritis, cholesterol emboli (Hansel stain)
Squamous epithelial > 5/LPF suggests contamination (inadequate collection)
Transitional epithelial From renal pelvis, ureter, bladder — large clumps suggest carcinoma
Renal tubular epithelial (RTE) ATN, nephrotoxic drugs, viral infections — significant even in small numbers

Casts (Tamm-Horsfall Mucoprotein)

Formed in distal tubules and collecting ducts. Always indicate RENAL pathology (except hyaline).

Hyaline Non-specific; exercise, dehydration, fever, CHF (up to 2/LPF normal)
RBC casts Glomerulonephritis (pathognomonic), vasculitis, malignant HTN
WBC casts Pyelonephritis, acute interstitial nephritis, lupus nephritis
Granular (muddy brown) Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN)
Waxy casts Chronic renal failure (advanced stasis, broad = renal failure casts)
Fatty casts Nephrotic syndrome (Maltese cross under polarized light)
Epithelial cell casts ATN, heavy metal poisoning, allograft rejection

Crystals

Acidic Urine Crystals

Uric acid Rhomboid/rosette, yellow-brown; gout, tumor lysis
Calcium oxalate Envelope/dumbbell shape; ethylene glycol poisoning, hyperoxaluria
Cystine Hexagonal (benzene ring); pathognomonic for cystinuria
Tyrosine/Leucine Fine needles/spheroids; severe liver disease

Alkaline Urine Crystals

Triple phosphate "Coffin-lid" shape; UTI with Proteus (struvite stones)
Ammonium biurate "Thorn apple"; old specimens
Calcium carbonate Dumbbell/spherical; usually non-pathological
Calcium phosphate Prisms/star-shaped; hyperparathyroidism

Organisms & Other Elements

Bacteria

Significant bacteriuria: ≥ 10⁵ CFU/mL (clean catch midstream). Rods (Gram-negative) vs cocci (Gram-positive).

Yeast

Candida spp. — oval, budding cells. Common in diabetes, immunosuppression, females.

Parasites

Trichomonas vaginalis (motile, flagellated). Schistosoma haematobium eggs (terminal spine).

Spermatozoa

May be seen post-coitus or retrograde ejaculation.

Mucus

Thread-like, low refractive index. Non-pathological (from urinary tract glands).

Oval Fat Bodies

RTE cells with absorbed lipids; Maltese cross on polarized light. Nephrotic syndrome.

Glomerular Disease Pattern

  • Proteinuria (often nephrotic range > 3.5g/24h)
  • RBC casts (pathognomonic)
  • Dysmorphic RBCs (acanthocytes)
  • Fatty casts & oval fat bodies (nephrotic)
  • Lipiduria with Maltese cross pattern

ATN Pattern

  • Muddy brown granular casts (hallmark)
  • Renal tubular epithelial (RTE) cell casts
  • Free RTE cells
  • Low specific gravity (isosthenuria ~1.010)
  • FENa > 2% (intrinsic renal failure)

UTI Pattern

  • Nitrite positive (Gram-negative organisms)
  • Leukocyte esterase positive
  • WBCs > 5/HPF (pyuria)
  • Bacteria visible on microscopy
  • WBC casts = pyelonephritis (upper UTI)
  • Alkaline pH with ammonia odor (Proteus mirabilis)

Diabetes Mellitus Pattern

  • Glycosuria (blood glucose > 180 mg/dL)
  • Ketonuria (DKA — acetoacetate detected)
  • Microalbuminuria (early nephropathy marker)
  • Polyuria with low specific gravity
  • Fruity odor (acetone in DKA)
  • Candida on microscopy (immunosuppression)

Collection Methods

Random Most common; convenient but variable concentration
First morning Best for routine; most concentrated, ideal for protein, nitrite, pregnancy testing
Clean-catch midstream Standard for culture; reduces contamination
24-hour collection Quantitative: protein, creatinine clearance, electrolytes, hormones
Catheterized Sterile; bedridden patients, ICU
Suprapubic aspiration Gold standard for sterile collection (infants, suspected anaerobes)

Specimen Handling & Timing

Analyze within 1 hour of collection (or refrigerate at 2–8°C, max 2h)
Delayed analysis effects ↑ pH, ↑ bacteria, ↓ glucose, ↓ ketones, ↓ bilirubin, lysis of casts/cells
Preservatives Boric acid (culture), toluene (24h chemistry), thymol, formalin (cytology)
Container Clean, dry, leak-proof; sterile for culture
Minimum volume 12 mL (routine UA); centrifuge 10–15 mL at 400g × 5 min

Quality Control

Dipstick storage Room temp, tightly capped, avoid humidity; discard if discolored
QC controls Run positive and negative controls each shift / new lot
Timing Read dipstick at exact manufacturer-specified times (60–120 sec for most pads)
Interference Ascorbic acid is the most common interfering substance (false-neg glucose, blood, nitrite)
Microscopy standardization KOVA system or standardized slide; report per HPF (400×) or LPF (100×)

Routine UA Workflow

  1. Assess physical properties — color, clarity, odor, volume
  2. Chemical dipstick — pH, SG, protein, glucose, ketones, blood, bilirubin, urobilinogen, nitrite, LE
  3. Centrifuge — 10–15 mL at 400g × 5 min; decant to 0.5–1 mL sediment
  4. Microscopic exam — scan 10 LPF then 10 HPF; report cells, casts, crystals, organisms
  5. Correlate findings — dipstick vs microscopy agreement; reflex to culture if indicated
  6. Report & flag — critical values (e.g., new RBC casts, massive proteinuria)
