Initial Workup (Time 0)

Laboratory

  • ABG/VBG (pH, pCO₂, HCO₃)
  • BMP (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, BUN, Cr, Glucose)
  • Serum ketones (β-hydroxybutyrate)
  • Serum osmolality (calculated + measured)
  • CBC with differential
  • Phosphate, Magnesium, Calcium
  • Lactate, LFTs, Lipase
  • HbA1c (if new diagnosis)
  • Urinalysis + Culture, Blood cultures

Calculated Values

Corrected Na⁺:
Na⁺ + 1.6 × [(Glucose - 100) / 100]
Anion Gap:
Na⁺ - (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻) → Normal: 8–12
Effective Osmolality:
2×Na⁺ + Glucose/18 → Normal: 275–295
Delta-Delta (Δ/Δ):
ΔAG / ΔHCO₃ → Mixed disorder if <1 or >2

Imaging & Other

  • ECG (hyperkalemia, MI)
  • CXR (infection, pulmonary edema)
  • Foley catheter (strict I/O)
Priority #1: Volume Resuscitation

Average fluid deficit in DKA: 5–9 L (100 mL/kg). Begin fluid resuscitation BEFORE insulin. Aggressive early hydration alone can reduce glucose by 35–70 mg/dL/hr.

Phase 1: Initial Resuscitation (Hour 0–1)

0.9% NaCl — 15–20 mL/kg/hr
≈ 1–1.5 L in first hour (for 70 kg adult)
⚠️ Max 50 mL/kg in first 4 hours; caution in CHF/CKD/elderly
If hemodynamically unstable:

Bolus 1L NS over 15–30 min, repeat as needed. Consider vasopressors if no response after 2–3L.

If cardiogenic shock/volume overload:

Use cautious 250–500 mL boluses. Early hemodynamic monitoring. Consider bedside echo.

Phase 2: Continued Replacement (Hours 1–24)

0.9% NaCl or 0.45% NaCl — 250–500 mL/hr
Rate based on hydration status, UOP, and corrected Na⁺
Na⁺ CHECK

Corrected Na⁺ HIGH or normal: Switch to 0.45% NaCl at 250–500 mL/hr

Corrected Na⁺ LOW: Continue 0.9% NaCl at 250–500 mL/hr

BG ≤200

When BG reaches 200–250 mg/dL: Add D5 to IV fluids (D5-0.45% NaCl) at 150–250 mL/hr

Target glucose: 150–200 mg/dL until DKA resolves. May use D10 if glucose drops too fast.

⚠️ Critical Safety Check

DO NOT start insulin if K⁺ < 3.3 mEq/L. Replace potassium first. Insulin drives K⁺ intracellularly → fatal hypokalemia → cardiac arrest.

IV Regular Insulin Protocol (Preferred for Moderate-Severe)

BOLUS 0.1 U/kg IV push (optional per ADA 2024)
DRIP 0.1 U/kg/hr continuous infusion
Alternative: 0.14 U/kg/hr WITHOUT bolus (equally effective per ADA)

Titration Protocol:

If BG not falling ≥50–75 mg/dL/hr: Double insulin infusion rate every hour until steady decline
When BG reaches 200–250 mg/dL: Reduce infusion to 0.02–0.05 U/kg/hr AND add D5 to IV fluids
Target maintenance: Keep BG 150–200 mg/dL until DKA resolution criteria met
💡 Key Principle: Do NOT stop insulin drip based on glucose alone

Insulin is treating KETOACIDOSIS, not hyperglycemia. Add dextrose to maintain glucose while continuing insulin until anion gap closes.

SubQ Rapid-Acting Insulin (Mild DKA Only)

Lispro/Aspart: 0.2 U/kg initially, then 0.1 U/kg q1–2h
Evidence: Non-inferior to IV in MILD DKA (Umpierrez et al.)
Eligible only if: pH >7.25, HCO₃ >15, alert, hemodynamically stable, no ICU requirement, reliable absorption (no shock/edema)

Transition: IV → SubQ Insulin

DKA Resolution Criteria (ALL must be met):

pH > 7.30
HCO₃ ≥ 18
AG ≤ 12
Patient eating

Overlap Rule: Give first dose of long-acting SubQ insulin (Glargine/Detemir) 2 hours BEFORE discontinuing IV insulin drip to prevent rebound ketoacidosis.

Dose: 0.5–0.8 U/kg/day total (basal-bolus split: 50% basal + 50% bolus). If insulin-naïve: start 0.4–0.5 U/kg/day.

Potassium Replacement — CRITICAL

⚡ #1 Cause of Death in DKA Management: Hypokalemia from insulin without adequate K⁺ replacement. Total body K⁺ depleted 3–5 mEq/kg despite normal/high serum K⁺.
K⁺ < 3.3 HOLD INSULIN
KCl 20–40 mEq/hr IV until K⁺ ≥ 3.3 mEq/L
Max peripheral: 10 mEq/hr per line. Central line required for >20 mEq/hr. Continuous cardiac monitoring mandatory.
K⁺ 3.3–5.3 REPLACE + START INSULIN
20–30 mEq KCl per liter of IV fluid
Goal: maintain K⁺ 4.0–5.0 mEq/L. Check q2h. Use KCl:KPhos 2:1 ratio if phos also low.
K⁺ > 5.3 HOLD K⁺, RECHECK q2h
Do not supplement. Start insulin (will lower K⁺).
ECG for peaked T-waves. Will drop rapidly with insulin + fluids. Recheck in 2 hours.

Bicarbonate — Very Limited Indications

⚠️ Routine use NOT recommended — may worsen hypokalemia, paradoxical CNS acidosis, delay ketone clearance, cerebral edema risk in pediatrics.

ONLY if pH < 6.9 (ADA 2024):

NaHCO₃ 100 mEq in 400 mL sterile H₂O + 20 mEq KCl at 200 mL/hr × 2 hours

Repeat q2h until pH > 7.0. Some guidelines also consider for pH 6.9–7.0 with hemodynamic instability or life-threatening hyperkalemia.

Phosphate Replacement

Routine replacement NOT recommended (no proven benefit in RCTs).

Replace if PO₄ < 1.0 mg/dL OR symptomatic:

KPhos 20–30 mEq/L in IV fluids (replaces part of KCl)

Symptoms: cardiac dysfunction, respiratory muscle weakness, hemolytic anemia. Monitor calcium (risk of hypocalcemia).

Magnesium

Often depleted in DKA. Replace if Mg²⁺ < 1.8 mg/dL or refractory hypokalemia.

MgSO₄ 1–2 g IV over 1–2 hours

Cerebral Edema — Most Feared Complication

Mortality 20–25%. More common in pediatric DKA but can occur in adults. Risk factors: new-onset DM, young age, severe acidosis, rapid osmolality correction, excessive free water, bicarbonate use.

Warning Signs:

  • • Headache, vomiting (after initial improvement)
  • • Altered mental status, irritability
  • • Cushing's triad (↑BP, ↓HR, irregular breathing)
  • • Papilledema, pupil changes
  • • Posturing, seizures
  • • Paradoxical decline in GCS during treatment

Immediate Management:

Mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg IV over 20 min
OR Hypertonic saline (3%) 5–10 mL/kg over 30 min

• Elevate HOB 30°

• Reduce IV fluids by 1/3

• Intubation if GCS ≤8 (avoid hyperventilation)

• Urgent CT head, neurosurgery consult

Other Complications

Hypokalemia

Most common lethal complication of treatment. Insulin + fluids drive K⁺ into cells. Monitor q2h. ECG changes: U-waves, flattened T-waves, ST depression.

Hypoglycemia

From excessive insulin without dextrose. Add D5/D10 when BG <250. Never stop insulin drip for glucose alone — treat the ketoacidosis.

ARDS / Pulmonary Edema

From aggressive fluid resuscitation. Risk: elderly, CKD, CHF. Monitor SpO₂, CXR. Use balanced crystalloids if concerned.

Hyperchloremic NAGMA

From large-volume NS. AG closes but pH stays low (non-gap acidosis). Use 0.45% NaCl or balanced solutions. Self-resolves in 24–48h.

VTE / Thromboembolism

DKA is prothrombotic (dehydration, immobility, inflammation). Consider prophylactic LMWH in adults unless contraindicated.

Rhabdomyolysis

From severe dehydration + hypophosphatemia. Check CK if oliguric/dark urine. Aggressive hydration, avoid nephrotoxins.
