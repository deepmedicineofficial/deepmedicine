Evidence-Based Clinical Reference

Antibiotic Prophylaxis

Clinical Reference Guide

Based on AHA/ACC, ASHP, IDSA, WHO & NICE Guidelines

What is Antibiotic Prophylaxis?

Antibiotic prophylaxis is the pre-emptive administration of antimicrobials to prevent infection in patients who are not yet infected but are at elevated risk due to planned procedures, immunocompromised states, or specific exposures. The goal is to achieve adequate tissue/serum drug concentration before bacterial contamination occurs.

Timing

Administer 30–60 min before incision. Vancomycin/fluoroquinolones: begin 120 min before. Repeat if surgery >2 half-lives or blood loss >1500 mL.

Spectrum

Target the most likely pathogens for the procedure site. Narrow-spectrum preferred. Broad empiric cover is NOT prophylaxis.

Duration

Single dose or ≤24 hours post-op for most surgical prophylaxis. Cardiac surgery: up to 48h. Longer ≠ better and increases resistance.

Core Principles (ASHP/IDSA/WHO)

1

Indication must be evidence-based. Not all surgeries require prophylaxis — Clean (Class I) wounds have SSI rates <2% without prophylaxis. Prophylaxis is indicated for clean-contaminated (Class II), implant/prosthesis surgery, and high-risk patients.

2

Correct drug at the correct dose. Weight-based dosing: Cefazolin 2g (3g if ≥120 kg). Underdosing in obese patients is the #1 modifiable failure point.

3

Redosing intraoperatively. Redose at 2× the half-life of the drug (Cefazolin q4h intra-op; Cefoxitin q2h). Also redose for significant blood loss (>1500 mL).

4

Discontinue promptly. Extended prophylaxis beyond 24h does NOT reduce SSI rates but DOES increase C. difficile risk, resistance emergence, and drug-related adverse events.

5

Consider local antibiogram. If institutional MRSA rates >10–20% for the specific procedure, add Vancomycin to (not replace) standard prophylaxis.

Surgical Prophylaxis by Procedure

ASHP/IDSA/SIS/SHEA Clinical Practice Guidelines 2013 (updated). β-lactam allergy alternatives listed.

Cardiac Surgery (CABG, Valve, Device Implant)

First Line: Cefazolin 2g IV (3g if ≥120 kg), within 60 min of incision. Redose q4h intra-op.

Penicillin Allergy: Vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV (begin 120 min before) OR Clindamycin 900 mg IV.

Duration: ≤48 hours post-op. Single dose may be adequate for low-risk.

Organisms: S. aureus, CoNS, Gram-negative bacilli.

If MRSA prevalence high: add Vancomycin to Cefazolin (do not replace). Consider mupirocin nasal decolonization + CHG bathing (NICE/WHO).

Orthopaedic (Arthroplasty, Spine, Open Fractures)

Joint Replacement/Spine with Hardware: Cefazolin 2g IV. Duration ≤24h.

Open Fractures:

  • Gustilo I–II: Cefazolin 2g IV q8h × 24h (some extend to 72h or until wound closure)
  • Gustilo III: Cefazolin + Gentamicin (or add Metronidazole for farm/soil contamination)
  • Gustilo IIIC (vascular injury): Add Penicillin G for Clostridial coverage

Allergy: Vancomycin or Clindamycin. For Gram-neg cover: Aztreonam or Aminoglycoside.

GI / Colorectal Surgery

Colorectal: Cefazolin 2g + Metronidazole 500 mg IV. OR Ertapenem 1g IV single dose.

Mechanical Bowel Prep + Oral Antibiotics (MBP+OA): Neomycin 1g + Erythromycin base 1g PO at 1 PM, 2 PM, 11 PM day before surgery. Strong evidence for reduced SSI when combined with IV prophylaxis (NNT ~10).

Appendectomy: Cefoxitin 2g IV single dose. OR Cefazolin + Metronidazole.

Cholecystectomy (lap, low-risk): NOT routinely recommended. Give if high-risk: acute, empyema, immunosuppressed, conversion to open.

Allergy: Clindamycin + Aminoglycoside or Aztreonam + Metronidazole.

Neurosurgery (Craniotomy, CSF Shunts)

Craniotomy: Cefazolin 2g IV. Duration ≤24h.

CSF Shunt/EVD Insertion: Cefazolin 2g IV single dose. Evidence for reduced shunt infection (OR 0.51).

Transsphenoidal: Cefazolin 2g IV ± nasal Mupirocin.

MRSA risk: Add Vancomycin. Allergy: Vancomycin or Clindamycin.

Urological Procedures

TURP / Cystoscopy with manipulation: TMP-SMX DS or Ciprofloxacin single dose (if urine sterile).

Prosthetic implant (penile, ureteral stent): Cefazolin 2g IV + Gentamicin.

Key: Always obtain pre-op urine culture. If bacteriuria, treat based on culture before elective procedure.

Obstetric & Gynecologic Surgery

Cesarean Section: Cefazolin 2g IV BEFORE skin incision (not after cord clamping — landmark change). Consider adding Azithromycin 500mg IV for unscheduled C-sections (C/SOAP trial: NNT=12 for SSI reduction).

Hysterectomy (all routes): Cefazolin 2g IV single dose.

Abortion/D&C: Doxycycline 200 mg PO pre-procedure (1st trimester surgical abortion).

Allergy: Clindamycin + Gentamicin. OR Metronidazole + Gentamicin.

Head & Neck / Clean-Contaminated

Clean-contaminated (through oral/pharyngeal mucosa): Cefazolin 2g + Metronidazole 500 mg IV. OR Ampicillin-Sulbactam 3g IV.

Duration: ≤24h post-op.

Clean head/neck (thyroid, parotid, no mucosal breach): Prophylaxis NOT indicated.

Infective Endocarditis Prophylaxis

AHA/ACC 2021 Guidelines — Significantly narrowed indications from 2007 update.

Prophylaxis ONLY for Highest-Risk Cardiac Conditions

  • • Prosthetic cardiac valves (mechanical, bioprosthetic, or homograft)
  • • Previous infective endocarditis
  • • Cardiac transplant recipients with valvulopathy
  • • Unrepaired cyanotic CHD, including palliative shunts/conduits
  • • Repaired CHD with prosthetic material/device — first 6 months after procedure
  • • Repaired CHD with residual defects at/adjacent to prosthetic patch or device

Prophylaxis NO LONGER Recommended For

  • • Mitral valve prolapse (even with regurgitation)
  • • Bicuspid aortic valve
  • • Calcific aortic stenosis
  • • Rheumatic heart disease
  • • Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
  • • All GI/GU procedures (even in high-risk cardiac patients)

Procedures Requiring Prophylaxis (in high-risk patients only)

Dental procedures involving manipulation of gingival tissue, periapical region of teeth, or perforation of oral mucosa.

Respiratory tract procedures involving incision/biopsy of respiratory mucosa (tonsillectomy, adenoidectomy, bronchoscopy with biopsy).

NOT indicated: GI endoscopy (even with biopsy), GU procedures, routine anesthetic injections through non-infected tissue.

Medical (Non-Surgical) Prophylaxis

Common indications encountered in ICU, Emergency, and General Medicine.

SBP Prophylaxis (Cirrhosis)

Primary (GI bleed): Ceftriaxone 1g IV × 7 days (or Norfloxacin 400 mg BID PO if able). All cirrhotic patients with GI bleed — reduces mortality.

Primary (low ascitic protein): Norfloxacin 400 mg/day PO (ascitic fluid protein <1.5 g/dL + either Na ≤130, Cr ≥1.2, BUN ≥25, or Child-Pugh ≥9 with bilirubin ≥3).

Secondary (prior SBP episode): Norfloxacin 400 mg/day PO indefinitely (or TMP-SMX DS daily). Reduces recurrence from ~70% to ~20%.

Meningococcal Meningitis Contacts

Rifampin: 600 mg PO q12h × 2 days (adults). Children: 10 mg/kg q12h.

Ciprofloxacin: 500 mg PO × 1 dose (adults, preferred for convenience).

Ceftriaxone: 250 mg IM × 1 dose (preferred in pregnancy).

Timing: Administer within 24h of index case identification. Effective up to 14 days post-exposure.

Rheumatic Fever Secondary Prophylaxis

Benzathine Penicillin G: 1.2 MU IM every 3–4 weeks (most effective).

Alternative: Penicillin V 250 mg PO BID or Sulfadiazine 1g PO daily.

Duration: RF without carditis: 5 years or until age 21. RF with carditis (no valvular disease): 10 years or until age 21. RF with persistent valvular disease: 10 years or until age 40 (some say lifelong).

PJP Prophylaxis (Pneumocystis jirovecii)

HIV: CD4 <200 cells/μL or CD4% <14% or oral candidiasis. TMP-SMX 1 SS or DS daily (also covers Toxoplasma).

Non-HIV: Prolonged corticosteroids (≥20 mg prednisone equivalent ≥4 weeks), organ transplant, certain chemotherapy regimens, temozolomide + RT.

Alternatives: Dapsone 100 mg/day PO (check G6PD first!), Atovaquone 1500 mg/day PO, or Pentamidine 300 mg inhaled monthly.

Post-Splenectomy / Asplenic Patients

OPSI (Overwhelming Post-Splenectomy Infection) carries 50–70% mortality. Prevention is critical.

Vaccinations (ideally 2 weeks pre-splenectomy): Pneumococcal (PCV20 or PCV15→PPSV23), Meningococcal (MenACWY + MenB), Hib, Annual Influenza.

Antibiotic Prophylaxis: Penicillin V 250 mg PO BID — at minimum 2 years post-splenectomy in adults; until age 5 (minimum) in children. Many experts recommend lifelong in high-risk patients.

Standby antibiotics: All asplenic patients should carry emergency Amoxicillin (or Levofloxacin if allergic) to self-administer at onset of febrile illness if unable to reach medical care within 2 hours.

Latent TB Treatment (Preventive Therapy)

3HP (preferred): Isoniazid + Rifapentine weekly × 12 doses (DOT). Shortest effective regimen.

4R: Rifampin daily × 4 months. Better completion rates than 9H.

9H (traditional): Isoniazid daily × 9 months (add Pyridoxine 25–50 mg/day to prevent neuropathy).

Monitor: LFTs at baseline and monthly if risk factors for hepatotoxicity (age >35, alcohol use, chronic liver disease, HIV).

Special Populations & Situations

Neonates — GBS Prophylaxis

Intrapartum: Penicillin G 5 MU IV loading → 2.5–3 MU q4h until delivery. OR Ampicillin 2g IV → 1g q4h.

PCN allergy: Cefazolin 2g IV → 1g q8h (low-risk allergy). Vancomycin 20 mg/kg q8h or Clindamycin (if susceptible) for high-risk allergy.

Indications: GBS+ screen at 36–37 weeks, GBS bacteriuria, prior infant with GBS disease, unknown status with risk factors (preterm <37 wks, ROM ≥18h, intrapartum fever ≥38°C).

Obesity (BMI ≥40)

Cefazolin: 3g if ≥120 kg (critical — underdosing is #1 failure point).

Vancomycin: 15 mg/kg actual body weight (max 2g/dose). Use actual body weight for loading.

Gentamicin: Use adjusted body weight (IBW + 0.4 × [ABW − IBW]).

Bariatric surgery: Cefazolin 3g (all patients, regardless of weight) + consider skin antisepsis with CHG.

Renal Impairment

Single-dose prophylaxis: No dose adjustment needed (drug is cleared after surgery).

Extended prophylaxis (≤24–48h): Adjust for CrCl <30 mL/min. Cefazolin: 2g q8h→q12h. Vancomycin: extend dosing interval, may not need 2nd dose.

Avoid: Aminoglycosides for prophylaxis in severe CKD unless absolutely necessary (use with TDM).

β-Lactam Allergy Assessment

True anaphylaxis (Type I): Avoid all β-lactams. Use Vancomycin + Aztreonam or Clindamycin + Gentamicin.

Non-severe (rash, remote history): Cephalosporins are safe (<1% cross-reactivity). Cefazolin is particularly low-risk (unique R1 side chain).

Best practice: Allergy history is wrong in >90% of cases. Penicillin skin testing can de-label safely and save Vancomycin use (antimicrobial stewardship).

MRSA-Colonized Patients

Known MRSA+ or institutional rate >10–20%: ADD Vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV to standard Cefazolin (do NOT replace — dual coverage).

Decolonization bundle: Mupirocin 2% nasal ointment BID × 5 days + CHG body wash. Strongest evidence in cardiac and orthopaedic surgery.

Neutropenic Patients

Fluoroquinolone prophylaxis: Levofloxacin 500 mg/day PO during expected neutropenia (ANC <100 for >7 days). ASCO/IDSA guideline.

Controversy: Reduces febrile neutropenia episodes but increases FQ resistance and C. difficile risk. Individualize based on local epidemiology.

Antifungal: Posaconazole or Micafungin during prolonged neutropenia in AML/MDS or allo-HSCT.

ICU & Emergency Medicine Pearls

Practical points for the intensivist and emergency physician.

🚨 Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) — Time-Critical

Rabies: Wound wash (soap + water 15 min) + HRIG (20 IU/kg infiltrated around wound) + Rabies vaccine (Day 0, 3, 7, 14). Non-immunized: 4 doses. Previously immunized: 2 doses (Day 0, 3), no HRIG. Do NOT delay — rabies is 100% fatal once symptomatic.

Needlestick/HIV: Start 3-drug ART within 2 hours (ideally <72h). Tenofovir/Emtricitabine + Dolutegravir × 28 days. Baseline + 6-week + 12-week HIV testing.

Hepatitis B: If source HBsAg+ and recipient unvaccinated: HBIG + vaccine series. If vaccinated with anti-HBs >10: no treatment needed.

Tetanus: Clean minor wound: Td/Tdap if >10 years since booster. Contaminated wound: Td/Tdap if >5 years + TIG if <3 doses of primary series or unknown.

🫁 VAP Prevention Bundle (not prophylaxis per se, but critical)

• HOB elevation 30–45° • Daily sedation interruption + SBT • Oral care with CHG 0.12% q6h • DVT prophylaxis • Stress ulcer prophylaxis (if indicated) • Subglottic secretion drainage ETT when available

Note: Selective digestive decontamination (SDD) and selective oropharyngeal decontamination (SOD) — used in some European ICUs — reduce ICU mortality (NNT ~20) but remain controversial due to resistance concerns. Not standard in North America/Asia.

🦠 Surgical Prophylaxis Failures — Top Causes

  1. Wrong timing — given >60 min before or after incision
  2. Underdosing in obesity — using standard 2g Cefazolin in 140 kg patient
  3. Failure to redose — long surgery without intra-op redosing
  4. Wrong drug — Cefazolin for colorectal (no anaerobic cover)
  5. Excessive duration — 5-day courses post-op (promotes resistance, not benefit)
  6. MRSA not covered — known colonization but no Vancomycin added

💊 Antimicrobial Stewardship Quick Wins

• Stop prophylaxis within 24h — this single intervention can reduce antibiotic-days by 30% in surgical wards.

• De-label penicillin allergy — 90% of "allergic" patients can safely receive β-lactams. Direct oral amoxicillin challenge is safe for low-risk history.

• Avoid vancomycin for convenience — reserve for documented MRSA colonization/infection or true anaphylaxis to β-lactams.

• Oral step-down: POET trial showed oral antibiotics non-inferior to IV for left-sided endocarditis. The IV-only dogma is changing.
