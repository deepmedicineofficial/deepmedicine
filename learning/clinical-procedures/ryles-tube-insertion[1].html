Ryle's Tube Insertion

Nasogastric Tube Placement — Clinical Procedure Guide

What is a Ryle's Tube?

A Ryle's tube (nasogastric tube / NG tube) is a thin, flexible tube made of polyvinyl chloride (PVC) or polyurethane, passed through the nose, down the oesophagus, and into the stomach. Named after John Alfred Ryle (1889–1950), Regius Professor of Physic at Cambridge.

Tube Specifications

  • Sizes: 10–18 Fr (adults); 5–8 Fr (paediatric)
  • Length: 105–125 cm
  • Material: PVC / Polyurethane / Silicone
  • Markings: Radiopaque line along entire length
  • Tip: Rounded, closed with lateral drainage eyes
  • Proximal end: Universal connector / funnel

Purposes

Decompression

Gastric decompression in intestinal obstruction, post-operative ileus, and gastric dilatation

Feeding

Enteral nutrition in patients unable to swallow — stroke, ICU, orofacial trauma

Therapeutic

Gastric lavage (poisoning), medication administration, and GI bleed monitoring

Indications

  • ● Intestinal obstruction — decompression
  • ● Post-operative gastric decompression
  • ● Enteral feeding (dysphagia, coma, ICU)
  • ● Gastric lavage (poisoning / drug overdose)
  • ● GI bleed — monitoring and lavage
  • ● Medication administration
  • ● Aspiration of gastric contents pre-anaesthesia
  • ● Diagnostic — gastric pH, cytology

Contraindications

Absolute

  • ✕ Choanal atresia
  • ✕ Base of skull fracture (cribriform plate #)
  • ✕ Oesophageal perforation / rupture
  • ✕ Oesophageal stricture (complete)

Relative

  • ⚠ Oesophageal varices (use with caution)
  • ⚠ Recent nasal / oesophageal surgery
  • ⚠ Coagulopathy / thrombocytopaenia
  • ⚠ Maxillofacial trauma
  • ⚠ Alkaline ingestion (risk of perforation)

Equipment Checklist

🧤Non-sterile gloves & apron
🔬Appropriate size NG tube (14–16 Fr adult)
💧Water-soluble lubricant (KY Jelly)
💉60 mL catheter-tip syringe
📏pH indicator paper (CE marked)
🏷️Adhesive tape / nasal bridle
🥤Glass of water with straw (if conscious)
🪣Kidney tray / emesis basin
🩺Stethoscope
👃Topical anaesthetic spray (Lidocaine 10%)
📎Drainage bag / spigot
🗑️Clinical waste bag & sharps container

Confirming Correct Placement

GOLD STANDARD: pH Testing of Aspirate

Aspirate gastric contents using a 60 mL syringe. Test pH using CE-marked indicator paper. pH ≤ 5.5 confirms gastric placement (NPSA Guidelines, UK).

Chest X-ray (if pH inconclusive)

Tube tip should be visible below the left hemidiaphragm in the gastric bubble area. Bisects the carina and follows the midline. This is the second-line confirmation method.

⚠ Unreliable Methods (DO NOT USE as sole confirmation)

  • ✕ Auscultation ("whoosh test") — can be misleading, air in oesophagus/lung sounds similar
  • ✕ Bubbling in water — does not distinguish gastric from bronchial
  • ✕ Absence of respiratory distress — tracheal placement may be asymptomatic initially
  • ✕ Visual appearance of aspirate alone

During Insertion

  • ⚡ Epistaxis — nasal mucosal trauma
  • ⚡ Coiling in oropharynx / oesophagus
  • ⚡ Tracheal intubation — coughing, desaturation
  • ⚡ Oesophageal perforation — rare but fatal
  • ⚡ Intracranial placement — in skull base #
  • ⚡ Vasovagal response — bradycardia, syncope
  • ⚡ Laryngospasm

Post-Insertion / Prolonged Use

  • ● Aspiration pneumonia — misplaced tube or reflux
  • ● Sinusitis / Otitis media — Eustachian tube obstruction
  • ● Nasal alar necrosis — pressure injury
  • ● Oesophageal erosion / stricture
  • ● Electrolyte imbalance — prolonged aspiration (↓Na⁺, ↓K⁺, ↓Cl⁻, metabolic alkalosis)
  • ● Tube blockage / kinking
  • ● Accidental displacement
