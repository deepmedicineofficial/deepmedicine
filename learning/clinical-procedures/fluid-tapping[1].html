Tapping Procedure Guide

Standard Protocol Reference — Thoracentesis & Paracentesis

Definition

Thoracentesis (Pleural Tapping) is the percutaneous aspiration of fluid from the pleural space for diagnostic or therapeutic purposes. It is one of the most commonly performed bedside procedures in internal medicine and critical care.

🔬 Diagnostic

  • • New-onset pleural effusion
  • • Undiagnosed effusion persisting >3 days
  • • Suspected empyema or malignant effusion
  • • Fever with pleural effusion
  • • Differentiation: Transudate vs Exudate

💊 Therapeutic

  • • Large symptomatic effusion (dyspnea)
  • • Massive effusion with mediastinal shift
  • • Empyema drainage
  • • Instillation of sclerosing agents
  • • Relief before definitive surgery

⛔ Absolute

  • • Uncooperative patient
  • • Uncorrected bleeding diathesis (INR >1.5, Platelets <50,000)
  • • Skin infection at puncture site
  • • Mechanical ventilation (relative — needs expert)

⚠️ Relative

  • • Small effusion (<1 cm on USG)
  • • Altered coagulation (use USG guidance)
  • • Bullous emphysema
  • • Single functioning lung on contralateral side
  • • Anticoagulant therapy

Sterile Kit

  • • Sterile gloves & gown
  • • Sterile drapes
  • • Antiseptic (Povidone-iodine / Chlorhexidine)
  • • Gauze pads

Needles & Syringes

  • • 18G & 20G needles
  • • 16G/18G IV cannula
  • • 20 mL & 50 mL syringes
  • • 3-way stopcock
  • • Connecting tubing

Anesthesia & Misc

  • • 2% Lignocaine (Lidocaine)
  • • 5 mL syringe + 25G needle
  • • Sterile collection bottles
  • • Adhesive dressing
  • • USG machine (if available)

Step-by-Step Procedure

1

Patient Preparation & Consent

Explain the procedure, risks, benefits, and alternatives. Obtain written informed consent. Verify identity, check coagulation profile (INR, platelet count). Review imaging (CXR / USG) to confirm effusion side and estimate volume.

2

Positioning

Patient sits upright, leaning forward on a bedside table with arms supported. If unable to sit: lateral decubitus with affected side down. Mark the site using USG guidance (preferred) or percussion (dullness).

3

Site Selection

Landmark: 1–2 intercostal spaces below the upper border of percussion dullness. Typically 7th–9th intercostal space, in the mid-scapular or posterior axillary line. Always insert at the upper border of the lower rib (to avoid the neurovascular bundle running under each rib).

4

Aseptic Technique

Perform hand hygiene. Don sterile gloves and gown. Clean the area with antiseptic in concentric circles (inside → out). Apply sterile drapes around the puncture site. Maintain sterile field throughout.

5

Local Anesthesia

Infiltrate 2% Lignocaine (5–10 mL) using a 25G needle. Anesthetize: skin → subcutaneous tissue → intercostal muscles → parietal pleura. Aspirate before injecting at each layer. Walk the needle over the superior border of the rib.

6

Needle Insertion & Aspiration

Insert the aspiration needle (16–18G cannula) attached to a syringe via a 3-way stopcock. Advance perpendicular to the chest wall at the upper border of the lower rib. Apply gentle negative pressure. A "give" is felt as the needle enters the pleural space and fluid is aspirated.

7

Fluid Collection

Diagnostic: Aspirate 50–100 mL into sterile containers. Send for: biochemistry (protein, LDH, glucose), cell count & differential, Gram stain & culture, cytology, ADA, and pH. Therapeutic: Drain slowly, maximum 1–1.5 L in one sitting to prevent re-expansion pulmonary edema.

8

Needle Removal & Dressing

Ask the patient to hold breath in expiration or hum. Remove the needle swiftly. Apply firm pressure with gauze, then adhesive occlusive dressing. Ensure no air entry occurs during removal.

9

Post-Procedure Care

Monitor vitals for 1 hour. Obtain a post-procedure CXR (erect PA) within 1–2 hours to rule out pneumothorax. Document: volume aspirated, fluid appearance, samples sent, patient tolerance. Watch for: dyspnea, chest pain, cough, hypotension.

Light's Criteria — Exudate vs Transudate

An effusion is an Exudate if it meets any ONE of the following:

Parameter Exudate Transudate
Pleural Protein / Serum Protein > 0.5 ≤ 0.5
Pleural LDH / Serum LDH > 0.6 ≤ 0.6
Pleural LDH > ⅔ upper limit of normal serum LDH ≤ ⅔ upper limit

Common Transudates

CCF, Cirrhosis, Nephrotic syndrome, Hypothyroidism, Meigs' syndrome

Common Exudates

Pneumonia (parapneumonic), TB, Malignancy, Pulmonary embolism, Rheumatoid, Pancreatitis

Definition

Paracentesis (Abdominal Tapping / Ascitic Tap) is the percutaneous aspiration of fluid from the peritoneal cavity. Performed for diagnostic evaluation of new-onset ascites or therapeutic large-volume removal in tense ascites.

🔬 Diagnostic

  • • New-onset ascites
  • • Suspected spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)
  • • Cirrhotic patient with fever, pain, or encephalopathy
  • • Malignant ascites evaluation

💊 Therapeutic

  • • Tense ascites causing respiratory compromise
  • • Diuretic-resistant ascites
  • • Large-volume paracentesis (LVP)
  • • Relief before procedures or imaging

Step-by-Step Procedure

1

Consent & Preparation

Informed consent. Verify coagulation (INR, platelets). Ensure bladder is emptied (catheterize if needed). Review imaging to confirm ascites. Patient supine with slight left lateral tilt.

2

Site Selection

Preferred site: Left lower quadrant — 2 cm medial and 2 cm superior to the ASIS (McBurney's point equivalent on the left). Avoid: surgical scars, distended veins, visible collaterals, and the inferior epigastric artery. USG guidance is strongly recommended.

3

Aseptic Technique & Anesthesia

Sterile prep identical to thoracentesis. Infiltrate 2% Lignocaine (5–10 mL) through skin, subcutaneous tissue, rectus sheath, and peritoneum. Use Z-track technique: pull skin 2 cm caudally before inserting needle to create a non-linear track (prevents persistent leak).

4

Needle Insertion

Insert 16–18G cannula using the Z-track technique. Advance with negative pressure until ascitic fluid is aspirated. Remove the needle stylet; leave the cannula in situ. Connect to 3-way stopcock and drainage bag/syringe.

5

Fluid Collection & Analysis

Diagnostic tap: 50–100 mL. Send for: cell count (TLC/DLC), albumin, protein, culture (inoculate blood culture bottles at bedside), Gram stain, cytology, ADA, glucose, LDH. Calculate SAAG (Serum-Ascites Albumin Gradient).

6

Large-Volume Paracentesis (LVP)

Can drain up to 5 L safely without albumin replacement. If >5 L drained: give IV Albumin 6–8 g per litre drained (e.g., 100 mL of 20% albumin per 2.5 L) to prevent paracentesis-induced circulatory dysfunction (PICD). Drain over 1–2 hours.

7

Post-Procedure Care

Apply sterile dressing. Place patient on the opposite side for 2 hours to prevent leak. Monitor vitals (BP, HR). Watch for: persistent leak, hypotension, peritonitis signs, hematoma. Document volume, appearance, and samples.

SAAG — Serum-Ascites Albumin Gradient

SAAG = Serum Albumin − Ascites Albumin

SAAG Value Interpretation Common Causes
≥ 1.1 g/dL Portal Hypertension Cirrhosis, CCF, Budd-Chiari, Portal vein thrombosis
< 1.1 g/dL Non-Portal Hypertension TB peritonitis, Malignancy, Pancreatic, Nephrotic syndrome

🚨 SBP Diagnostic Criteria

Ascitic fluid PMN (Neutrophil) count ≥ 250/mm³ = Diagnostic of SBP. Start empirical antibiotics immediately (IV Cefotaxime 2g q8h or Ceftriaxone). Positive ascitic fluid culture further confirms diagnosis.

Thoracentesis Complications

Serious

  • • Pneumothorax (most common serious)
  • • Hemothorax (intercostal artery injury)
  • • Re-expansion pulmonary edema (>1.5 L drained rapidly)
  • • Organ laceration (liver/spleen)
  • • Air embolism (rare)

Minor

  • • Pain at puncture site
  • • Subcutaneous hematoma
  • • Vasovagal reaction
  • • Local infection / cellulitis
  • • Dry tap (loculated fluid)

Paracentesis Complications

Serious

  • • PICD — Paracentesis-Induced Circulatory Dysfunction (hypotension, renal failure)
  • • Bowel perforation
  • • Hemorrhage (inferior epigastric artery)
  • • Secondary bacterial peritonitis

Minor

  • • Persistent ascitic fluid leak
  • • Local pain and bruising
  • • Abdominal wall hematoma
  • • Vasovagal episode
  • • Wound infection

Prevention of Complications

Thoracentesis

  • ✅ Use USG guidance (reduces pneumothorax by 70%)
  • ✅ Never drain >1.5 L at once
  • ✅ Insert over superior rib border
  • ✅ Stop if patient coughs persistently or feels chest tightness
  • ✅ Post-procedure CXR mandatory

Paracentesis

  • ✅ Use Z-track technique (prevents persistent leak)
  • ✅ Albumin infusion if draining >5 L
  • ✅ Avoid surgical scars & visible collaterals
  • ✅ USG-guided site selection
  • ✅ Empty bladder before procedure
