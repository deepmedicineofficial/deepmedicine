Basic Life Support — C-A-B Approach

1

Scene Safety & Response Check

  • • Ensure scene is safe for rescuer and victim
  • • Tap & Shout: "Are you okay?" — tap shoulders firmly
  • • Check for normal breathing (gasping is NOT normal breathing)
  • • If unresponsive + no normal breathing → Activate EMS + Get AED
  • • Lone rescuer: use phone on speaker, start CPR immediately
2

Pulse Check (Healthcare Provider)

  • • Carotid pulse for adults/children; Brachial pulse for infants
  • • Check for no more than 10 seconds
  • • If no definite pulse within 10 sec → Begin CPR
  • • If pulse present but no breathing → Rescue breathing (1 breath every 6 sec for adults; every 2–3 sec for infants/children)
C

Circulation — Chest Compressions

  • • Heel of hand on lower half of sternum
  • • Rate: 100–120/min | Depth: ≥ 5 cm but ≤ 6 cm
  • • Allow complete chest recoil — don't lean on chest
  • • Minimize interruptions: < 10 seconds pauses
  • • Rotate compressors every 2 minutes (5 cycles of 30:2)
  • • Hard & Fast, Push & Release — "Stayin' Alive" tempo
A

Airway — Open the Airway

  • • Head-tilt, Chin-lift maneuver (primary technique)
  • • Jaw-thrust if suspected cervical spine injury
  • • Suction if visible obstruction; consider oropharyngeal/nasopharyngeal airway
B

Breathing — Ventilation

  • • 30:2 ratio (compressions:breaths) without advanced airway
  • • Each breath over 1 second, visible chest rise
  • • Avoid excessive ventilation — causes gastric insufflation, ↑ intrathoracic pressure, ↓ venous return
  • • With advanced airway: Continuous compressions + 1 breath every 6 sec (10 breaths/min)
  • • BVM with O₂ reservoir at 15 L/min → delivers ~100% FiO₂
D

Defibrillation — AED Use

  • • Use AED as soon as available
  • • Power ON → Attach pads → Analyze → Shock if advised
  • • Resume CPR immediately after shock — do NOT recheck rhythm
  • • Reanalyze every 2 minutes
  • • Anterior-lateral pad placement (right infraclavicular + left axillary)
  • • Special: Water → dry chest; Patches → remove; Pacemaker → place pad 1 inch away

High-Quality CPR Checklist

✅ Rate 100–120/min
✅ Depth ≥ 5 cm (adult)
✅ Full chest recoil
✅ Minimize interruptions (<10 sec)
✅ Avoid excessive ventilation
✅ Rotate compressors q 2 min
✅ Chest compression fraction > 60%
✅ EtCO₂ target ≥ 10 mmHg (if monitored)

ACLS — Advanced Cardiac Life Support

Code Team — Role Assignment

🎯 Team Leader
Directs all actions, assigns roles, makes rhythm/drug decisions, maintains situational awareness
💪 Compressor
Delivers high-quality compressions, rotates every 2 min, calls out depth/rate
🫁 Airway Manager
BVM ventilation → advanced airway (ETT/SGA), monitors EtCO₂, ensures O₂ flow
💉 IV/IO & Medications
Establishes IV/IO access, prepares & administers drugs, flushes with 20 mL NS + elevates extremity
⚡ Defibrillator/Monitor
Operates defibrillator, monitors rhythm, charges during compressions, announces shock
📋 Recorder/Timer
Documents events, timestamps drugs & shocks, calls 2-min intervals, tracks epinephrine q3–5 min

Algorithm Flow:

  1. CPR 2 min → Rhythm check → VF/pVT identified
  2. Shock #1: Biphasic 120–200 J (or manufacturer dose) / Mono 360 J
  3. CPR 2 min immediately → IV/IO access during compressions
  4. Rhythm check → Still VF/pVT → Shock #2
  5. CPR 2 min + Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO (then q 3–5 min)
  6. Rhythm check → Still VF/pVT → Shock #3
  7. CPR 2 min + Amiodarone 300 mg IV/IO (1st dose)
  8. Continue cycle: CPR → Rhythm check → Shock → Drug
  9. Amiodarone 150 mg (2nd dose) can be given
  10. Consider Lidocaine if Amiodarone unavailable: 1–1.5 mg/kg → 0.5–0.75 mg/kg

Key point: Epinephrine starts AFTER 2nd shock. Amiodarone starts AFTER 3rd shock. Every shock is followed by IMMEDIATE CPR — never delay compressions to recheck pulse.

Algorithm Flow:

  1. CPR 2 min → Rhythm check → Asystole/PEA identified
  2. Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO ASAP (then q 3–5 min)
  3. CPR 2 min → Rhythm check
  4. If rhythm changes to VF/pVT → Switch to shockable algorithm
  5. If still asystole/PEA → Continue CPR + Epi q 3–5 min
  6. Aggressively search for H's & T's

Key difference: Epinephrine given ASAP (not after 2nd shock). No antiarrhythmics. No shocks. Treat the CAUSE — PEA often has a reversible etiology.

Confirm Asystole: Check leads, gain/amplitude, check in 2 leads. "Asystole protocol" — confirm it's not fine VF.

EtCO₂ (Capnography)

  • • Target: ≥ 10 mmHg during CPR
  • • If <10 → improve compression quality
  • • Sudden rise to ≥ 40 mmHg → suggests ROSC
  • • Best physiologic indicator of CPR quality
  • • Also confirms ETT placement

Arterial Line (if placed)

  • • Diastolic pressure ≥ 20 mmHg target
  • • Correlates with coronary perfusion pressure
  • • If <20 → optimize compressions
  • • Consider vasopressors

Cardiac Arrest Rhythms

SHOCKABLE

Ventricular Fibrillation (VF)

  • • Chaotic, irregular electrical activity — no organized QRS
  • • No cardiac output — immediate defibrillation needed
  • • Most common initial rhythm in witnessed adult cardiac arrest
  • • Best prognosis of all arrest rhythms when treated early
SHOCKABLE

Pulseless V-Tach (pVT)

  • • Wide, regular QRS complexes — rate usually >150 bpm
  • • Monomorphic or polymorphic morphology
  • • No palpable pulse → treat as cardiac arrest
  • • Polymorphic VT (Torsades) → Magnesium 1–2 g IV
NON-SHOCKABLE

Asystole

  • • Flatline — no ventricular electrical activity
  • • Confirm in 2 leads; check connections, gain
  • • Do NOT defibrillate — no rhythm to reset
  • • Worst prognosis — focus on reversible causes (H's & T's)
NON-SHOCKABLE

PEA (Pulseless Electrical Activity)

  • • Organized electrical activity but no palpable pulse
  • • Can look like any rhythm — sinus, bradycardia, etc.
  • • Most commonly has a reversible cause
  • • Aggressively treat H's & T's — often the best chance at ROSC

Code Medications

Epinephrine (Adrenaline)

First-Line Vasopressor
💉

Dose & Route

  • • 1 mg IV/IO every 3–5 minutes
  • • Follow with 20 mL NS flush
  • • Elevate extremity for 10–20 sec

Timing

  • • VF/pVT: After 2nd shock
  • • Asystole/PEA: As soon as possible
  • • α-1 effects → ↑ coronary perfusion pressure

Amiodarone

Antiarrhythmic — Class III
⚡

Dose

  • • 1st dose: 300 mg IV/IO bolus
  • • 2nd dose: 150 mg IV/IO
  • • Pediatric: 5 mg/kg IV/IO (max 300 mg)

Indication

  • • Refractory VF/pVT — after 3rd shock
  • • K⁺/Na⁺/Ca²⁺ channel + β-blocker activity
  • • Prolongs action potential duration

Lidocaine

Antiarrhythmic — Class Ib
💊
  • • Alternative to amiodarone if unavailable
  • • 1st dose: 1–1.5 mg/kg IV/IO
  • • 2nd dose: 0.5–0.75 mg/kg (every 5–10 min; max 3 mg/kg)

Other Code Medications

Magnesium Sulfate

1–2 g IV/IO diluted in 10 mL D5W

Indication: Torsades de Pointes

Sodium Bicarbonate

1 mEq/kg IV

Indication: Hyperkalemia, TCA overdose, metabolic acidosis

Calcium Chloride 10%

20 mg/kg IV (max 2 g) — via central line preferred

Indication: Hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia, Ca²⁺ blocker OD

Atropine

1 mg IV q 3–5 min (max 3 mg)

Indication: Symptomatic bradycardia (NOT routine in arrest)

Vascular Access Priority

1st: IV (peripheral — antecubital preferred) → 2nd: IO (proximal tibia, humeral head) → 3rd: Central line (if skilled)

All drugs: Follow with 20 mL NS bolus + elevate extremity. IO doses = IV doses.

Post-ROSC Management

ROSC = Return of Spontaneous Circulation

Signs: palpable pulse, sudden sustained ↑ EtCO₂ (typically ≥40 mmHg), arterial pressure wave, spontaneous breathing/movement.

🫁 Ventilation & Oxygenation

• Titrate FiO₂ to SpO₂ 92–98% — avoid hyperoxia (causes oxidative injury)

• Ventilation rate: 10 breaths/min

• Target EtCO₂ 35–45 mmHg (normocapnia) — or PaCO₂ 35–45

• Avoid hypocapnia (↓ cerebral blood flow) and hypercapnia

• Secure advanced airway if not already placed; confirm with waveform capnography

❤️ Hemodynamic Optimization

• Target SBP ≥ 90 mmHg or MAP ≥ 65 mmHg

• IV fluid bolus (1–2 L NS) for hypotension

• Vasopressors if needed: Norepinephrine (0.1–0.5 mcg/kg/min) or Epinephrine infusion (0.1–0.5 mcg/kg/min)

• 12-Lead ECG — if STEMI → emergent PCI (even if comatose)

• Dopamine: 5–20 mcg/kg/min (alternative vasopressor)

🧠 Targeted Temperature Management (TTM)

• For comatose patients (no meaningful response to verbal commands) after ROSC

• Target: 32–36°C for ≥ 24 hours (AHA 2020: select and maintain constant temp)

• Methods: IV cold saline, surface cooling devices, endovascular catheters

• Actively prevent fever (≥37.7°C) for at least 72 hours

• Rewarm slowly: 0.25–0.5°C/hour

• Prevents secondary neuronal injury from reperfusion

🔬 Investigations Post-ROSC

Immediate

  • • 12-Lead ECG
  • • ABG (pH, lactate, electrolytes)
  • • CBC, BMP, Troponin
  • • Coagulation profile
  • • Chest X-ray
  • • Glucose — treat hypoglycemia

Consider

  • • CT Head (if no clear cardiac cause)
  • • CT Pulmonary Angiography (suspected PE)
  • • Echocardiography
  • • Coronary angiography (STEMI/high suspicion ACS)
  • • EEG monitoring (seizure detection)
  • • Toxicology screen

🧪 Neuroprognostication

• Wait ≥ 72 hours after ROSC (or after rewarming from TTM) before prognostication

• Multimodal approach: clinical exam (pupillary/corneal reflexes, motor response), EEG, SSEP, MRI, NSE biomarkers

• No single test should be used alone to predict poor outcome

Reversible Causes — The H's & T's

Systematically consider these during every cardiac arrest — especially PEA and asystole. Identifying and treating the cause is often the only path to ROSC.

H The H's

Hypovolemia

Clues: Hx of bleeding/trauma, flat neck veins, narrow pulse pressure on monitor

Tx: IV crystalloid bolus, blood products (PRBCs), stop hemorrhage source, massive transfusion protocol

Hypoxia

Clues: Cyanosis, airway obstruction, Hx of respiratory disease

Tx: Effective ventilation with 100% O₂, confirm advanced airway placement, waveform capnography

Hydrogen Ion (Acidosis)

Clues: DKA, renal failure, prolonged arrest, sepsis, diarrhea

Tx: Sodium Bicarbonate 1 mEq/kg IV; ventilation (respiratory compensation); treat underlying cause

Hyper/Hypokalemia

Clues: Renal failure, dialysis patient, medications (ACEi, K-sparing diuretics), ECG changes

Tx Hyperkalemia: Calcium Chloride/Gluconate, NaHCO₃, Insulin + Glucose, Kayexalate, emergent dialysis

Tx Hypokalemia: IV KCl replacement (10–20 mEq/hr peripherally; faster via central line)

Hypothermia

Clues: Exposure history, cold skin, core temp <35°C

Tx: Active rewarming (warm IV fluids 40°C, warm blankets, body cavity lavage in severe cases). May need prolonged resuscitation — "not dead until warm and dead"

T The T's

Tension Pneumothorax

Clues: Unilateral absent breath sounds, tracheal deviation, distended neck veins, trauma/ventilated patient

Tx: Needle decompression (2nd ICS, midclavicular or 4th/5th ICS, anterior axillary) → chest tube

Tamponade (Cardiac)

Clues: Beck's triad (muffled heart sounds, JVD, hypotension), PEA with narrow QRS, pericardial effusion on echo

Tx: Pericardiocentesis (needle or surgical) → definitive surgical repair. Bedside echo is diagnostic gold standard

Toxins / Overdose

Clues: Pill bottles, medication Hx, toxidromes, young patient, unknown cause arrest

Tx: Specific antidotes — Narcan (opioids), NaHCO₃ (TCA/Na-channel blockers), Intralipid (local anesthetic toxicity), Flumazenil (benzos — use with caution), Digibind (digoxin). Prolonged CPR may be warranted

Thrombosis — Pulmonary (PE)

Clues: Recent surgery/immobilization, DVT Hx, RV dilation on echo, sudden PEA arrest

Tx: Systemic thrombolytics (tPA 50 mg IV bolus during CPR or alteplase); consider surgical/catheter-directed embolectomy. Continue CPR for 60–90 min after lytic administration

Thrombosis — Coronary (MI)

Clues: Hx of CAD/chest pain, STEMI on monitor, age/risk factors

Tx: Emergent coronary angiography + PCI (even during ongoing CPR in select cases — cath lab CPR). Consider fibrinolytics if PCI unavailable

💡 Quick Memory Aid

5 H's:

Hypovolemia, Hypoxia, Hydrogen ion, Hyper/Hypokalemia, Hypothermia

5 T's:

Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade, Toxins, Thrombosis (pulmonary), Thrombosis (coronary)
